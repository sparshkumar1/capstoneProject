\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}
The empirical results separate two architectural dimensions often combined under the multi-agent label: coordination and specialization. Introducing the bounded coordination layer altered the observed difficulty trajectory relative to the frozen single-controller baseline ($B_0$), whereas reorganizing identical functional responsibilities into specialized versus unified components did not alter matched per-turn decisions. This distinction is consistent with the behavioral trajectory change arising from the introduction and admission of auxiliary recommendations, while specialization primarily reorganizes responsibility and state boundaries without modifying decision outputs under equivalent deterministic rules.

The ablation matrix isolates where this behavioral divergence enters the decision loop. Ablating the Strategy Agent ($M_2$) caused the multi-agent system to reproduce the baseline trajectory identically across all 160 replay sessions, whereas removing the Coaching Agent ($M_3$) or the unidirectional Strategy-to-Coaching peer message ($M_6$) left the difficulty trajectory unchanged ($M_3 \equiv M_1$, 160/160 sessions). The matched open-loop comparison reinforces this finding: when presented with identical observations, question sequences, and heuristic rules, the specialized two-agent architecture ($M_{\text{split}}$) and the unified controller ($M_{\text{unified}}$) produced identical normalized decisions across all 1,280 turns ($\mathbb{P}(\text{agree}) = 1.0$). In this architecture, these comparisons indicate that Strategy influenced difficulty transitions and probing through its pathway to committed state, whereas Coaching, which is deliberately restricted from authority over difficulty, did not; this reflects the tested topology and deterministic agents, not a general property of agent architectures.

Specialization therefore provides an explicit responsibility and state boundary rather than a measured behavioral advantage, at minor dispatch ($0.0440$ vs $0.0401$\,ms) and serialization ($0.0092$ vs $0.0038$\,ms) overheads; relative to $B_0$, the coordination layer added a $+1.04$\,ms median per-turn overhead (47.7 calls per session).

The primary behavioral control point instead resides at the arbitration boundary where proposals are admitted into the authoritative decision path. The arbitration ablation ($M_4$) produced trajectory collapse toward a volatile regime, while the threshold sweep (XC) indicated that the confidence boundary $\tau$ controls proposal admission. At $\tau = 0.0$, low-confidence default proposals override PPO broadly, collapsing the trajectory toward the unarbitrated regime ($1.000$ reversals, $0.014$ PPO agreement) while guardrail bypass remains zero because other tested safeguards remain active. This identifies confidence-gated proposal admission as a major control point for the observed trajectory under the tested replay conditions. At $\tau = 0.9$, the threshold exceeds all realized heuristic confidences, preventing Strategy proposals from superseding PPO and reverting trajectories to $B_0$. The behavioral plateau across $\tau \in [0.5, 0.7]$ reflects the discrete confidence values generated by the tested heuristic ($0.70$ and $0.80$), consistent with $\tau$ functioning as an operational control on proposal admission rather than a globally tuned hyperparameter.

This mechanism also clarifies how trajectory smoothness must be evaluated. While $M_1$ exhibited fewer level changes ($15.12 \to 12.45$; cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI $[-4.50, -0.24]$) and directional reversals ($11.74 \to 9.87$; cluster CI $[-3.12, -0.27]$) than $B_0$, lower volatility is not intrinsically equivalent to superior adaptation. In condition $M_4$, removing arbitration produced an even flatter trajectory while abandoning PPO alignment ($0.014$) and allowing guardrail bypass. Lower volatility can thus reflect the suppression of adaptive adjustments rather than improved decision quality. A similarly smooth trajectory arises when PPO is unavailable ($B_7$: $2.675$ level changes per session), which again shows that low volatility alone is not a quality criterion. Stability represents an observable systems metric, not a standalone pedagogical quality criterion. The evidence for the trajectory change is also bounded in strength: the primary cluster-level interval excluded zero, but leave-one-cluster-out analysis showed that the magnitude was sensitive to the small set of eight structural clusters.

The authority topology explains why distributed responsibility does not require decentralized state mutation. Strategy and Coaching maintain encapsulated private ledgers and emit typed proposals, while \texttt{InterviewOrchestrator} remains the sole authoritative writer of session state. Under targeted fault injection, structural schema validation and forbidden-key scanning intercepted malformed and unauthorized mutations, optimistic concurrency rejected stale proposals, and arbitration contained adversarial-valid proposals (0/40 bypasses vs 38/40 unarbitrated). However, structural and lexical validation does not establish semantic correctness: lexical grounding accepted unsupported paraphrased credit in 57/57 applicable authored cases, demonstrating layered containment rather than complete semantic verification.

Contradicted predictions show that components can remain behaviorally latent: ablating PPO ($M_7$) altered only 4 of 160 trajectories (2.5\%) because Strategy heuristics largely agreed with the rule fallback, and canonical guardrails altered 0 of 160 paths despite 11.8\% synthetic grid disagreement because the divergent states were unreached under neutral replay defaults. Evaluations that measure only component presence risk misidentifying which mechanisms the evaluated distribution actually exercises.

Several boundaries delimit these findings. The replay evaluation does not establish that reduced volatility improves candidate learning, retention, confidence, or interview placement. The deterministic agents provide no evidence regarding autonomous LLM-based agents in open-ended natural language interaction, and the architecture does not constitute multi-agent reinforcement learning because the PPO policy is frozen and the auxiliary agents are not jointly trained.
