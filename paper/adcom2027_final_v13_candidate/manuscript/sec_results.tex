\noindent\textbf{Execution Invariants and Baseline Transparency.} Every evaluated condition completed all 160 sessions, maintained strict evaluator authority (committed scores equaled recorded evaluator output in 100\% of sessions), and enforced valid difficulty bounds in $[1, 5]$ with transitions of at most one level per step (A1--A3; exact Clopper--Pearson 95\% lower bound for 160/160: 0.977~\cite{cp34}). In the silent-agent condition ($M_2$), difficulty, score, and question trajectories reproduced the baseline ($B_0$) identically in 160 of 160 sessions (A4), demonstrating that the coordination infrastructure is completely transparent when agents remain inactive.

\noindent\textbf{Trajectory Divergence and Oscillation Smoothing.} Enabling multi-agent coordination ($M_1$) altered the difficulty trajectory relative to the frozen baseline ($B_0$) in \textbf{all 160 replay sessions (100\%)} (A7; Table~\ref{tab:xapaired}). The stateful Strategy Agent smoothed difficulty oscillations across turns: mean level changes fell from 15.12 to 12.45 per session (point difference $-2.67$, naive session bootstrap 95\% CI $[-3.19, -2.13]$), and mean reversals fell from 11.74 to 9.87 per session ($-1.88$, naive CI $[-2.28, -1.46]$). Agreement with the uncoordinated PPO action dropped from 0.626 to 0.400 (overall PPO agreement fraction 0.774), with a Strategy proposal superseding PPO before the guardrail layer in an average of 11.9 decisions per session.

\noindent\textbf{Cluster Dependence and Proactive Probing Sensitivity.} Because the 160 sessions reuse 8 underlying replay clusters, treating sessions as independent underestimates parameter uncertainty. A post-hoc cluster bootstrap over the 8 replay clusters (Field and Welsh~\cite{fw07}, $B = 10{,}000$) yields wider, defensible intervals: level changes \phchgmean{} \phchgci{}, reversals \phrevmean{} \phrevci{}, and PPO action agreement \phagrmean{} \phagrci{}. The negative sign for level changes and reversals is shared across 7 of the 8 replay clusters.

Proactive probing added an average of \phfumean{} turns per session (naive CI $[+0.34, +0.85]$, cluster CI \phfuci{}). However, cluster-level decomposition reveals that this probe increase is present in \textbf{only \phfusame{} of the 8 replay clusters} ($k=1$); in the remaining seven clusters, proactive probing added exactly 0.00 turns. A leave-one-cluster-out (LOCO) sensitivity analysis confirms that omitting cluster $k=1$ shifts the level-change delta to $-3.76$ (CI $[-4.81, -2.78]$), whereas omitting any of the other seven clusters causes the 95\% interval to include zero. Thus, while the directional tendency toward oscillation reduction is robust across clusters, the specific magnitude and extra-turn gains are highly cluster-dependent and cannot be generalized as an unconditional property.

\noindent\textbf{Proposal Distribution.} Across the 160 sessions, the Strategy Agent generated \scTotal{} proposals: \scRemEight{} \emph{remediate} proposals (confidence 0.80), \scRecEight{} and \scRecSeven{} \emph{recover} proposals (confidence 0.80 and 0.70), and \scSteady{} \emph{steady} proposals (confidence 0.45). Notably, the Strategy Agent emitted zero \emph{stretch} proposals (\scStretch{}), indicating that during replay the candidate trajectories never maintained the sustained high-performance criteria ($3\text{ scores} \ge 0.60$, $\text{EMA} \ge 0.70$) required to trigger proactive difficulty increases. The agent operated exclusively to stabilize or lower difficulty.

\noindent\textbf{Coordination Overhead.} Introducing the multi-agent layer increased pooled per-turn latency from \xaBzeroMed{}\,ms ($B_0$) to \xaMoneMed{}\,ms ($M_1$) at the median (end-to-end orchestration overhead $+1.04$\,ms, 95\% CI $[+1.00, +1.08]$\,ms) and from \xaBzeroHigh{}\,ms to \xaMoneHigh{}\,ms at the 95th percentile, executing an average of 47.7 agent calls per session. This end-to-end orchestration overhead reflects the broader coordination path across the turn lifecycle, encompassing multi-agent invocation, peer observation passing, contract checking, and arbitration logic.

\noindent\textbf{Decomposing Arbitration via Threshold Sensitivity (XC).} In the unarbitrated condition ($M_4$), where all arbitration checks were simultaneously disabled, trajectory dynamics collapsed: level changes fell to 2.225 per session, PPO agreement plummeted to 0.014, and guardrail bypass occurred (Table~\ref{tab:xa}). Experiment XC isolates the specific gating mechanism by varying only the confidence threshold $\tau \in [0.0, 0.9]$ while keeping all other safety checks active (Table~\ref{tab:xc}).

Setting $\tau = 0.0$ alone reproduced the $M_4$ trajectory collapse (level changes \xctauzerochg{}, PPO agreement \xctauzeroagr{}) with \textbf{zero guardrail bypass} in any session (C1). Because confidence is min-clamped at 0.45 for unevidenced \emph{steady} proposals, setting $\tau = 0.0$ allows uncalibrated default proposals to override PPO on virtually every turn. This confirms that the collapse in $M_4$ is caused specifically by the missing confidence threshold, not by absent guardrails or step bounds. Behavior remained completely flat across $\tau \in [0.5, 0.8]$ (12.450 level changes, 0.774 agreement) because realized evidenced confidences are discrete steps (0.70 and 0.80). At $\tau = 0.9$, the threshold exceeds all possible heuristic confidences ($\max = 0.80$), completely silencing the Strategy Agent and reverting trajectories to the baseline ($B_0$). All \xcsupported{} registered XC predictions were supported.

\noindent\textbf{Peer Information Exchange ($M_5$).} Ablating the unidirectional peer message from Strategy to Coaching ($M_5$) resulted in difficulty trajectories 100\% identical to the full configuration ($M_1$) across all 160 sessions (A5). The peer message does not feed back into Strategy decision-making; its role is strictly to condition Coaching presentation (switching depth to \emph{detailed} during \emph{recover}, \emph{brief} during \emph{stretch}, and attaching remediation tags during \emph{remediate}).

\noindent\textbf{Contradicted Prediction A9 (PPO Fallback Interaction).} Pre-registered prediction A9 posited that disabling the learned PPO policy would alter $\ge 50\%$ of session paths. Empirically, disabling PPO ($M_7$) altered difficulty trajectories in only \textbf{4 of 160 sessions (2.5\%)}. Inspection of orchestrator traces reveals that under replay conditions, the rule-based Strategy Agent heuristic predominantly reaches the identical difficulty action as the non-RL heuristic fallback, demonstrating high behavioral alignment between the heuristic rules and the learned policy fallback.

\noindent\textbf{Contradicted Prediction A10 (Guardrail Divergence).} Pre-registered prediction A10 posited that evaluating the system with canonical guardrails ($B_{0c}$) would alter at least one trajectory path. In replay, \textbf{zero paths differed (0/160)}. While the two implementations disagree on 11.8\% of synthetic state grid points (4,728 of 39,930), the divergent boundary regions require extreme acoustic hesitation or low voice confidence. Because replay data contain neutral acoustic defaults, the divergent guardrail states are never reached.

\noindent\textbf{Trajectory Invariance under Functional Decomposition.} A central motivation for multi-agent architecture is the assumption that role specialization produces different decision behavior. To test this hypothesis, we examine Condition $M_3$, where the Coaching Agent is disabled while the Strategy Agent remains active. The implementation establishes a structural invariant implied by the architecture's authority and information topology: \texttt{CoachingPlan} contains no difficulty-control fields, \texttt{arbitrate\_difficulty()} does not inspect the coaching plan, and inter-agent communication is unidirectional from Strategy to Coaching through \texttt{TurnObservation.peer}. Under this architecture, Coaching execution does not provide an information pathway back to the Strategy difficulty decision.

Consistent with this structural property, the matched replay experiment produced identical difficulty trajectories in all 160 evaluated sessions ($M_3 \equiv M_1$, Table~\ref{tab:xapaired}), with identical level changes (12.45), directional reversals (9.87), and score progressions (A5).

\noindent\textbf{Decision Equivalence under Matched Replay.} When evaluated on the identical sequence of observation inputs under matched heuristic rules, a standalone unified controller (\texttt{UnifiedCoachAgent}) synthesizing both strategy and coaching outputs produced identical proposals across all 160 sessions (1,280 turns):
$$\mathbb{P}(\text{Decision}_{\text{unified}} = \text{Decision}_{\text{split}}) = 1.0$$
Under the tested deterministic replay configuration, the split and unified architectures produced identical decisions and difficulty trajectories. The result indicates that architectural decomposition alone did not alter the implemented decision policy in this evaluation setting.

\noindent\textbf{Architectural Characteristics and Operational Overhead.} As synthesized in Table~\ref{tab:specialization}, the results indicate that, under the tested configuration, specialization primarily manifests as an architectural decomposition rather than a change in the resulting decision policy, introducing measurable orchestration overhead:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{State Decoupling (Architectural Design Property):} Strategy and Coaching maintain disjoint memory structures, establishing distinct schema boundaries for difficulty adaptation and pedagogical tracking. While this separation does not yield empirical decision divergence or measured developer productivity gains in replay, it isolates state models across domains.
\item \emph{Operational Cost (Measured Empirical Outcome):} Specialization incurs measurable runtime penalties across three distinct measurement scopes: (i)~\emph{End-to-end orchestration overhead}: $+1.04$\,ms median turn latency ($B_0$ 0.55\,ms vs $M_1$ 1.59\,ms; $+1.85$\,ms p95; 47.7 calls/session), measuring the full orchestrator turn path and arbitration; (ii)~\emph{Component dispatch latency}: a narrower dispatch measurement of $0.0440$\,ms per turn for split vs $0.0401$\,ms for unified ($-8.9\%$, saving $-0.0039$\,ms); and (iii)~\emph{Serialization micro-benchmark}: validating two Pydantic models requires $0.0092$\,ms vs $0.0038$\,ms for one model ($-58.7\%$, saving $-0.0054$\,ms). Crucially, the serialization micro-benchmark isolates schema validation and explains only a minor fraction of the broader $+1.04$\,ms end-to-end orchestration overhead.
\end{itemize}
Under the tested configuration, specialization introduces decoupled state ledgers at the expense of directly measured component dispatch latency, serialization micro-benchmark overhead, and end-to-end orchestration runtime.
