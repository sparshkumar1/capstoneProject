\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}
We investigated multi-agent coordination and agent specialization within an orchestrated technical interview preparation system. Introducing specialized Strategy and Coaching agents with private ledgers, typed contracts, and unidirectional peer messaging alters difficulty trajectories across all 160 replay sessions, smoothing level changes ($-2.67$) and reversals ($-1.88$) relative to the frozen single-policy PPO baseline, while adding $+0.59$ proactive probe turns driven by specific conceptual weakness clusters.

Crucially, our analysis challenges the assumption that agent specialization inherently alters decision/trajectory behavior. Under deterministic replay, an ablated coaching condition and an equivalent unified agent produce identical difficulty trajectories ($\mathbb{P}(\text{agree}) = 1.0$, $1{,}280/1{,}280$ turns). Specialization manifests as an architectural decomposition rather than an altered decision policy, introducing measurable end-to-end orchestration overhead ($+1.04$\,ms median per-turn latency) alongside serialization micro-benchmark differences ($0.0092$\,ms vs $0.0038$\,ms) and decoupled state ledgers. Through threshold sweeps and cluster-bootstrap replay, this work provides an evidence-based foundation for bounded-authority multi-agent systems for adaptive technical-interview applications.
