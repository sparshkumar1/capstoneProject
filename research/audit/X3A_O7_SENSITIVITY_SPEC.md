## 1. Estimands: registered versus conditional (nothing is silently replaced)

| Label | Estimand | Random factors | Fixed factors | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **REG** (registered) | Mean persona-level paired MAE difference, averaged over the persona population *and* the population of PPO trainings | personas (40 grid) **and** training seed (5) | none (evaluation seeds averaged within cells) | **Primary, pre-registered, unchanged** |
| **A** | Mean paired difference **for these five frozen checkpoints**, over the persona population | personas | training checkpoints (the five) | Secondary robustness; conditional estimand |
| **B** (conditional seed-level sensitivity interval) | Mean of the five seed-level (persona-averaged) paired differences, i.e. the mean difference for a hypothetical new training run of the frozen recipe **conditional on this 40-persona grid** | training seed (n = 5, df = 4) | personas (the 40 grid cells) | Secondary robustness; **not a replacement CI for REG**; normality cannot be assessed |
| **C** | Influence of each checkpoint on the point estimate and on the registered-procedure classification | not an inferential estimand | - | Stability description |

Points that must be stated wherever these are reported:
- **REG remains the primary preregistered estimand, with training seed modelled as a random factor. It is not replaced, weakened or re-labelled by A, B or C, and it is not silently swapped for a fixed-checkpoint estimand.** A fixed-checkpoint interval (A) is a labelled conditional estimand only.
- **Extrapolation beyond the five observed checkpoints is limited.** Five trainings from a non-reproducible recipe (`P3-C017/C018`) cannot support strong inference about the population of trainings. A random-factor interval built on n = 5 seeds is itself only a coarse statement about that population, and no statement about untrained or differently trained policies is supported by X3-A.
- The protocol treats training seed as a "second random factor" (persona = unit). The five checkpoints are the frozen study's trainings (seeds 42/123/456/789/999). The registered interpretation rule already reads "no detectable PPO contribution beyond the shield **in these checkpoints**", i.e. the *scope of the claim* is these checkpoints while the *interval* is built as if trainings were random. This tension is real and is not resolved here by choosing one estimand. It is exposed by reporting REG (primary), A and B side by side with their estimands labelled.
- The "population of trainings" is a modelling device: the frozen recipe trained against a single unseeded candidate (`P3-C017/C018`), so retraining does not regenerate the checkpoints. The random-seed reading therefore refers to a hypothetical population, not to a reproducible procedure.
- The 40 personas are a full-factorial grid (5 types x 8 skills), not a random sample. Treating them as random (REG, A) or fixed (B) are both modelling choices; the spec reports both.
- A and B are **components**, not alternatives to the registered CI: A isolates persona uncertainty with seeds held fixed, B isolates seed uncertainty with personas held fixed. Neither is a valid CI for REG, and their intervals must not be added or combined into a new interval. No "combined" analysis is added to this spec.

## 2. Common preparation (applies to A, B, C)

Source: the stored `research/confirmatory/X3-A/results/sessions.csv` (SHA-256 `dd7eb669e1ffc60957f254e330185d69edf276d578b0b0029efdcf3880f1bcd8`), read only.

1. **Key-set assertions first (addresses O8).** For stratum `grid`: condition `PPO | G` must contain exactly the training seeds {42, 123, 456, 789, 999}, each with all 40 persona IDs and the same set of 20 evaluation seeds per persona, with no duplicate (condition, training seed, persona, evaluation seed) key; condition `Constant-Same | G` (training seed `-`) must contain the same 40 personas and the same 20 evaluation seeds. Any failure stops the analysis and is reported; nothing proceeds.
2. **Cell means.** `M_PPO[p, s]` = mean over the 20 evaluation seeds of column `mae` for persona p and seed s; `M_CS[p]` = the same for Constant-Same+G. Evaluation seeds are **averaged within cells in every analysis and are never resampled**; they are treated as within-cell replication (common-random-numbers simulator noise stays inside the cell means, as in the registered analysis).
3. **Paired matrix.** `D[p, s] = M_PPO[p, s] - M_CS[p]`, a 40 x 5 matrix. Derived vectors: `Dbar_p = mean_s D[p, s]` (persona-level, length 40) and `Delta_s = mean_p D[p, s]` (seed-level, length 5). Point estimate `mean(D)` equals the registered -0.03504548324999993 (checked in step 4).
4. **Precondition R0 (independent reproduction gate).** R0 must reproduce the registered point and CI **before A, B or C can run**, and it must be **implemented independently of `x3a_analyze.py`**:

## 4. The crossed-factor bootstrap issue (stated with its assumptions)

The registered interval resamples the row factor (personas) and the column factor (training seeds) independently and averages the resampled cells, i.e. a two-way "pigeonhole"/product bootstrap. Its statistical behaviour depends on assumptions and cannot be summarised as "over-" or "under-estimates":
- Owen (2007, *The pigeonhole bootstrap*) and Owen & Eckles (2012, *Bootstrapping data arrays of arbitrary order*) analyse resampling each factor of a crossed array independently. As I understand it, their results, derived under a crossed-random-effects model with both factors sampled from large populations, a mean-type statistic and (near-)balanced arrays, indicate that this scheme is generally **conservative** (variance biased upward) in that setting, with the excess attributable mainly to how the residual/interaction component is counted, while the row-only and column-only components are shrunk by finite-sample factors. These statements are conditional on those assumptions, and the citations must be verified by ChatGPT before any use (they are not in Zotero and were not checked here).
- This design departs from those assumptions: only **5** columns, so percentile intervals from very few clusters are separately prone to being too narrow; the 40 personas form a fixed factorial grid rather than a sample; the comparator (Constant-Same) has no seed dimension, so all seed-to-seed variation comes from PPO; and the estimand's seed factor is hypothetical (Section 1).
- Consequently the **net direction of any bias in the registered interval is not known a priori** (an upward tendency from crossed resampling and a downward tendency from few clusters may partly offset). This is the reason for specifying sensitivity analyses rather than assuming either direction. No claim is made here that the registered interval is too narrow or too wide.

## 5. Distance-to-boundary arithmetic: descriptive only

Registered values: point -0.0350, lower limit -0.0818, boundary -0.12. The lower limit lies 0.0468 below the point; the boundary lies 0.0850 below it; their ratio is about 1.8. This may be quoted **only** as a descriptive statement of how far the registered lower limit is from the margin. It is **not a sensitivity result** because it (i) takes the registered interval as given and rescales it symmetrically around the point, which is not how a different estimand or a different variance estimator changes an interval; (ii) evaluates no alternative uncertainty model; (iii) says nothing about coverage or bias; and (iv) invites the inference "robust because far from the boundary", which the analyses above must be allowed to confirm or contradict. Likewise `sqrt(5/4) = 1.118` is **not** used: it is a heuristic finite-cluster factor for one variance component, not evidence about the whole interval, and no robustness conclusion may rest on it.

## 6. No selection among analyses; treatment of the registered result

1. A, B and C are fixed here **before execution** and all three are reported in full whatever they show. No analysis is added, dropped, re-parameterised, or re-labelled after results exist. Not included by design: BCa or studentised bootstraps, variance-component decompositions, leave-one-persona-out, evaluation-seed resampling, other margins, TOST/90% intervals, combined interval. If ChatGPT wants any of these, they must be added to this specification **before** the run.
2. No analysis is chosen because it preserves (or fails to preserve) Equivalence; no single "preferred" robustness interval is nominated.
3. The registered classification (Equivalent) remains the result of record for the registered estimand in every circumstance. REG stays the primary preregistered estimand with training seed modelled as a random factor; no outcome of A, B or C promotes a fixed-checkpoint (or any other) estimand to primary, and any wording must state that extrapolation beyond the five observed checkpoints is limited.

**If classifications differ.** Report the registered result first and unchanged; then report each secondary classification with its estimand label and the reason (for example: A narrower because seeds are fixed; B wider because n = 5 gives 4 degrees of freedom). A disagreement is a *limitation to be stated*, not a correction: the wording becomes, for example, "equivalent within +/-0.12 under the registered two-factor estimand; not established under [estimand X]", and any claim about the frozen checkpoints is worded accordingly. The X3-B trigger and the registry status of `X3A-C001` are not changed by a secondary result. If all classes agree, the wording is "registered result unchanged under three labelled robustness checks", not "robust".
