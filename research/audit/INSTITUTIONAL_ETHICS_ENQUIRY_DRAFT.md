## Draft message (institution)

**Subject:** Guidance requested: human ratings of technical answers in a software-evaluation study (existing data and one planned round)

Dear [NAME / COMMITTEE],

I am writing to ask for guidance on how to document and, if required, review the use of human raters in my research project, **before** any manuscript is submitted. I am not asserting that any approval is or is not needed; I am asking what is required.

**The project.** PREPAIred is a technical-interview preparation system. One paper evaluates an automatic scoring component by comparing its scores with human ratings of written answers to technical questions (data structures, algorithms, systems).

**Existing data (already collected).**
- Three raters and one adjudicator scored 64 short candidate answers to 8 technical questions using a fixed rubric and a fixed scale. The answers were **constructed by the study team** (they are not answers from real candidates) and contain no personal data about their authors. [User: confirm this is accurate before sending — the repository shows a single authoring script but does not document how the answer texts were produced.]
- The rating files, frozen protocol and final consensus scores are stored in the project repository and are cryptographically hash-pinned. The repository contains no personal data about the raters.
- The repository does **not** currently contain: the identities and qualifications of the raters and adjudicator, how they were recruited and their relationship to the team, the instructions each received, when the files were sent and returned, any consent or acknowledgement record, any compensation terms, or any institutional determination. I am now trying to recover whatever of this exists elsewhere. I will not create or backdate any record; anything written now will be dated now and labelled as retrospective.
- [User to state, truthfully, anything known: e.g., who the raters were in general terms, whether they agreed by email, whether anything was paid.]

**Planned data collection (one round).** To strengthen the study, I plan **one** new, larger, fully documented rating round: about [20–30 — to be set by a precision analysis] new technical questions with [8] candidate answers each (written by members of the study team or generated by a recorded language model and not selected on the basis of the scoring system's output), rated blindly by at least three raters, each rating every item, using the same fixed rubric. Raters would see only the question, the answer and the rubric; they would not see the scoring system's output. I will record consent, roles, dates, time spent and any tool use at the time. [If applicable: real candidates' answers are **not** planned; if that changes I will ask again.]

**Questions I would be grateful for guidance on.**
1. For the **existing** ratings: is an ethics determination, exemption statement or other documentation required or advisable, and can it be obtained or issued retrospectively? If a retrospective statement is not acceptable, how should the limitation be disclosed?
2. For the **planned round**: is prior review or a determination required? What must the consent/information sheet contain? Is compensation (if any) permitted, and how should it be recorded?
3. What identifying information about raters may appear in a paper or repository (I plan to use coded IDs only and keep identities off-repository)?
4. Are there institutional requirements for data storage, retention and sharing of rating files?
5. Who is the appropriate person to act as principal investigator / ethics contact for the study?
6. What should be stated in the ethics/data statement of a submission in each case?
7. Is there a timeline I should know about, so that the planned round is not delayed?

**What I will do in the meantime.** I will not start the planned round, will not contact prospective raters, and will not submit any manuscript that relies on the human ratings, until I have your guidance.

I can share the frozen protocol, the rubric, the data file, and the (non-personal) documentation on request.

Thank you for your time.
