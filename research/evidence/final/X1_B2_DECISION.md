# Decision on X1-B2 (behavioural stress with a varying answer) — NOT RUN (2026-09-20)

**What B2 would have to support.** A claim such as "Qwen-driven behavioural changes cannot circumvent technical authority" or "multimodal/adaptive behaviour remains controlled under LLM stress". Those are claims about the whole answer-to-difficulty loop with a varying candidate answer, the real evaluator, the follow-up channel and the difficulty controller acting together.
**What Paper 1 now claims (`PAPER1_FINAL_CLAIM_MATRIX.md`).** Only the narrower statement that, with the evaluator result held fixed, the tested score/difficulty/best-answer/controller observables did not depend on LLM-authored feedback text (P1-B1), plus the documented, untested follow-up channel (P1-B3). That statement is what B1 (fixed-answer invariance) measures; B2 is outside its scope.
**Why not run.** (1) No retained claim needs it. (2) It would need a new registered protocol, a sample-size rationale that does not exist (no sample size is to be invented), a varying-answer design whose oracle is not the exact-equality rule, and a decision about how to treat the follow-up channel — none of which can be settled validly in the remaining time. (3) The follow-up channel is a documented LLM→evaluator dependency, so "cannot circumvent" would be false as stated; a B2 result could only characterise it, which is future work, not a gate.
**Recorded as future work:** a registered B2 with (a) a defined claim about the follow-up channel, (b) a varying-answer design and an oracle that tolerates legitimate score variation, (c) a stated, justified sample size. Until then the paper must not use "cannot", "isolated", "zero authority" or any statement about follow-up-generated questions.
