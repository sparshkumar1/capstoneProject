# E1 — PPO Persona-Diversity Development Experiment: Protocol V2

**Status: design only. Not registered, not executed, not tagged, not committed, not pushed. No code has been written. No training has been run.** V1 is preserved unmodified below this file's own history; every correction here is substantive (a genuine leakage fix, a re-derived and now evidence-verified intervention design, and a corrected evidentiary claim), not cosmetic.

## 0. What changed from V1, and why each change was necessary

Three real defects were found on reassessment against `PPO_TRAINING_EXPERIENCE_AUDIT.md` and `RL_GUARDRAIL_COVERAGE_DECOMPOSITION_REPORT.md`, plus one new piece of direct evidence that sharpens the intervention design:

1. **A development/confirmatory leakage defect in V1 (real, now fixed — §3).** V1 §3 described the development phase as running "freely" on the same 5 confirmatory personas/seeds (`1001–5005`) already used to evaluate every existing frozen policy, then called a subsequent run on those *same* seeds "confirmatory." Once a test set has been inspected during development, evaluating on it again is not independent confirmation, regardless of intent. **Fixed** by introducing a genuinely fresh, never-before-used confirmatory seed set, disjoint from every seed used anywhere in this project to date, held untouched until every design and selection decision is frozen.
2. **An evidentiary overreach in how the (unattributable) training log's findings were carried forward (real, now corrected — §1).** The prior audit's summary said PPO had "learned the Harder direction reasonably well in-distribution." That log cannot be attributed to the five frozen checkpoints (no seed column, ~2.4× their combined training budget, untracked/gitignored). The correct, narrower claim — retained below — is that **the reward function demonstrably provides frequent, strongly-differentiated positive signal for correct Harder decisions under the one training-candidate configuration the code is known (by independent static-code reading, finding N1) to use** — a fact about the *reward's behavior under that configuration*, not proof that *the specific frozen policies* internalized it.
3. **The skill-only intervention was not checked against what actually drives the demonstrated blind spot, and it should have been (real, now corrected — §2).** A direct, exact re-query of the same frozen replay data used for the guardrail-coverage report (run below, not merely assumed) shows this precisely:

   > **All 120 of the demonstrated guardrail-free "oracle wants Harder" turns belong to exactly one persona, `lucky_guesser` (mean perf=0.966, mean conf=0.299, mean hes=0.804), and every single one (120/120, 100%) satisfies guardrail G6's own "nervous-expert" exclusion clause (`conf<0.40 AND hes>0.60`) — which is precisely why no guardrail ever covers these turns.** Neither `struggling_junior` nor `overconfident_fail` (clamped to low performance) ever has a guardrail-free "oracle wants Harder" turn at all — for them the relevant oracle action is always Easier, not Harder.

   This means the entire demonstrated Harder-blind-spot opportunity is concentrated in a persona whose defining trait is a **confidence/hesitation decoupling from performance** (high performance, low confidence, high hesitation) — exactly the dimension that varying `skill` alone under `persona="normal"` (which always keeps confidence and hesitation naturally correlated with performance) **cannot produce, at any skill value.** V1's skill-only design (Condition B) was therefore not well-matched to the specific defect it was proposed to fix. This is now corrected in §2, with the exact numbers above kept as the design's justification rather than a plausibility argument.

## 1. Training-evidence interpretation, corrected

**Retained, and still valid:** under the training-time reward/guardrail code (`rl/env/interview_env.py`, `rl/guardrails.py`), when the tested candidate's oracle target is "Harder," resolving to "Harder" is rewarded at +0.552 on average and failing to is penalized at −0.676 on average (from the training-configuration-consistent log) — a real, frequent (39,652 instances), strongly differentiated signal. This describes the **reward function's behavior under the one training-candidate configuration (`skill=0.6, persona="normal"`) known, by static code reading alone (`X3_0B_STATIC_CODE_NOTE.md`, finding N1), to be the only one ever used.**

**Not claimed, and explicitly withdrawn if it was implied before:** that the five frozen checkpoints (seeds 42/123/456/789/999) actually learned this behavior. The log that produced the numbers above cannot be attributed to those specific runs (no seed identifier; ~2.4× their combined recorded training budget; untracked and gitignored by the project's own convention). **The correct, load-bearing claim for this protocol is narrower: the reward design does not *prevent* learning "Harder" correctly and actively reinforces it when the relevant state is visited — so if the frozen checkpoints fail at it anyway, the most likely reason is that they never visited the relevant state at all (distribution shift), not that the reward failed to reinforce it when visited.** This is retained as the leading hypothesis motivating E1, not treated as an established, complete cause of the evaluation-time failure — the intervention in §2 is designed to be able to falsify it, not just confirm it.

## 2. The intervention, reassessed and redesigned

### 2.1 Does skill-only diversity (V1's Condition B) address the demonstrated defect? — No, by direct evidence, not assumption

Per §0's finding: the demonstrated Harder blind spot is 100% concentrated in a conf/hes-decoupled state pattern that skill variation under `persona="normal"` structurally cannot reach (that branch keeps `conf ≈ perf − 0.15` and `hes ≈ 1 − conf`, both plus small independent noise, at every skill level). **Running V1's Condition B alone would very likely leave the demonstrated blind spot exactly as it is**, even if it produced other, unrelated benefits (e.g., for `struggling_junior`/`overconfident_fail`-type low-performance states, which is where the *Easier* side of the state space — not Harder — has its own, structurally different opportunity, per the companion decomposition report).

### 2.2 A carefully parameterized behavioral-diversity intervention — feasibility, checked

The simulator supports this without touching any frozen file: `SimulatedCandidate.answer_question()` returns a plain dict (`performance_score, confidence_score, hesitation, response_time`); nothing requires the caller to use those values unmodified. A **new, external, compositional wrapper** (not a subclass of `SimulatedCandidate`, not a modification of it) can hold an internal `SimulatedCandidate(skill=s, persona="normal", seed=...)`, call its `answer_question()` unchanged, and apply one **disclosed, bounded, single-parameter decoupling offset** to the returned values before passing them on:

```
conf' = clip(conf - k, 0, 1)
hes'  = clip(hes + k, 0, 1)
```

for a decoupling strength `k` drawn from a small, fixed, disclosed grid — **not** a new persona archetype, not a claim about any real behavioral pattern, a single synthetic knob that pushes confidence down and hesitation up together, in the one direction the demonstrated defect requires, leaving performance itself untouched (so this never becomes a second skill axis in disguise).

### 2.3 The controlled comparison — two arms, not one, chosen to isolate what actually matters

Per the instruction not to combine changes indiscriminately, and to prefer a design that can identify *what* helps, **both arms are run, as a paired, comparably-cheap comparison, not a single bet**:

| Arm | Candidate construction | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **B1 (skill-only, unchanged from V1)** | `SimulatedCandidate(skill=s, persona="normal")`, `s` cycling through a fixed 17-point grid over [0.15, 0.90] | The (now evidence-weak) hypothesis that skill breadth alone closes the blind spot. Retained deliberately as a **negative-control ablation**: §0's finding predicts this arm should show little or no improvement on the specific Harder-blind-spot mechanism check, even if it helps elsewhere. |
| **B2 (skill + hesitation-decoupling, new)** | The §2.2 wrapper around `SimulatedCandidate(skill=s, persona="normal")`, `(s, k)` cycling through a 6×4 grid: `s ∈ {0.20, 0.35, 0.50, 0.65, 0.80, 0.90}`, `k ∈ {0.0, 0.15, 0.30, 0.45}` (24 cells; at `s=0.90, k=0.45`, the resulting states land in the same region as `lucky_guesser`'s observed `perf≈0.97, conf≈0.30–0.40, hes≈0.60–0.70`, confirmed by direct calculation from the natural `conf≈perf−0.15`/`hes≈1−conf` relationships) | The corrected, evidence-targeted hypothesis: that exposing PPO to genuine performance/confidence/hesitation decoupling — not just performance-level breadth — is what the demonstrated defect requires. |

**Why both, and why this is still bounded:** B1 and B2 share the identical wrapper mechanism, the identical reward/state/action/hyperparameters, and a comparable number of distinct training cells (17 vs. 24) — the incremental engineering cost of B2 over B1 is one additional grid dimension and one three-line post-processing step in the same new file, not a second implementation effort. Running both, at the identical (primary) training budget, costs roughly double B1's compute alone (§5) — still on the order of minutes, not hours — and is the only way to attribute any observed effect to *which* axis of diversity mattered, rather than asserting it.

**Isolation guarantee, unchanged from V1 and re-verified:** neither arm touches the reward function, the 6-D state definition, the 3-action space, or any SB3 hyperparameter. Both arms differ from Condition A, and from each other, only in how `InterviewEnv`'s per-episode candidate is constructed.

**Explicitly not assumed:** that either B1 or B2 improves pooled MAE. §0/§2.1 make a specific, falsifiable prediction (B1 should not move the Harder-blind-spot mechanism check; B2 might) — this is a prediction to be tested, not a promised outcome, and §4's criteria are built to detect and report a null or negative result for either or both arms honestly.

## 3. Development/confirmatory split — corrected, leakage removed

**The defect, stated precisely:** V1 proposed inspecting Condition B's results on the same `EVAL_SEEDS = [1001,2002,3003,4004,5005]` already published for Condition A/Constant-Same+G/Heuristic+G during "development," then calling a subsequent run on those *same* seeds "confirmatory." Because the simulator is deterministic given a seed, and because those five seeds' outcomes for the *existing* policies are already fully known and published, using them again for Condition B's own development inspection does not itself leak anything new about Condition B's *own* confirmatory result — but explicitly re-labeling a look at exactly those seeds as "confirmatory" afterward is not independent evaluation, and the instruction is right to reject it outright.

**The fix — is a genuinely independent confirmatory evaluation possible here? Yes, directly, no limitation to declare.** `EVAL_SEEDS` is nothing more than an RNG seed controlling `SimulatedCandidate`'s noise stream and the environment's initial-difficulty draw (`run_session_trajectory`, `rng = np.random.RandomState(eval_seed)`); any new integer seed produces a fresh, never-before-computed trajectory. The simulator places no obstacle in the way of genuine seed-level independence. (**Residual limitation, disclosed and not fixable by this protocol:** the five *persona identities* — `struggling_junior, overconfident_fail, normal, lucky_guesser, nervous_expert`, each a fixed skill + fixed hand-written behavioral clamp — remain the same fixed set defined in `PERSONA_TARGETS`; no new eval seed changes which personas are being tested, only the noise realization within each. This is an existing, already-disclosed limitation of the whole P3/system design, not something E1 introduces or is expected to resolve.)

**Corrected split, precisely specified:**
- **Development seeds/scenarios (freely inspected, used for every design and selection decision):** the existing, already-published `EVAL_SEEDS = [1001,2002,3003,4004,5005]`, run against every arm (Condition A's existing checkpoints, B1, B2, both training budgets if the extended arm is triggered) exactly as V1 already specified for its (mislabeled) "development" phase. All checkpoint/config selection — which of B1/B2 to carry forward, whether the extended training budget is needed — is decided **here, and only here**, before the confirmatory seeds are ever touched.
- **Confirmatory seeds (locked, touched exactly once, after every decision above is frozen):** a **new, disjoint set never used anywhere in this project's P3/X3 history**, proposed as `EVAL_SEEDS_CONFIRMATORY = [6006, 7007, 8008, 9009, 10010]` (chosen simply as the next unused block in the same numbering pattern as `1001…5005`, disclosed as an arbitrary but fixed choice, not tuned).
- **Confirmatory run composition — paired, identical scenarios for every policy, run once:** on `EVAL_SEEDS_CONFIRMATORY`, evaluate **all four policies together, in the same pass**: Condition A's existing frozen PPO+G checkpoints (re-evaluated fresh on the new seeds — this does not retrain or modify them, only replays them, exactly as `x3_0c_replay.py` already does for the existing seeds), the one selected Condition-B arm (B1 or B2, chosen from development evidence alone), Constant-Same+G, and Heuristic+G. **This is the first time in this project that Condition A's own checkpoints are evaluated on seeds other than 1001–5005** — necessary so that Condition A is not given a home-field advantage of five-times-replayed seeds while B is judged on a first look.
- **Historical baselines preserved exactly as published:** the existing `1001–5005` numbers for Condition A, Constant-Same+G, and Heuristic+G are **not modified, recomputed, or overwritten** anywhere by this protocol — they remain in `research/experiments/paper3/` untouched, and are reported alongside the new confirmatory numbers as the historical reference, explicitly labeled as such, never conflated with the new, independent confirmatory result.
- **Training seeds** remain as in V1: the same 5 values (`42,123,456,789,999`) for every arm's PPO optimization randomness, with the persona/behavior-sampling stream independently offset (`seed+10000`) — this is about training-time randomness, not evaluation-time seeds, and was not itself a source of the leakage; retained unchanged.

## 4. Performance criteria — strengthened and re-anchored to the corrected split

**Primary objective, unchanged and re-affirmed: the existing, unmodified MAE (`target_tracking_error`) — exactly as currently implemented and reported.** No redefinition.

**Reported for every policy, on both the development seeds (diagnostic only) and the locked confirmatory seeds (decisive):**
1. Pooled MAE and the equally-weighted mean over the 5 personas.
2. Per-persona MAE, with particular attention to `lucky_guesser` (the persona §0 shows carries the entire demonstrated Harder-blind-spot opportunity) and to `struggling_junior`/`overconfident_fail` (the personas the companion decomposition already showed carry the concentrated Easier-side opportunity).
3. Action distributions and the guardrail-coverage/override/oracle-agreement decomposition, re-applied per arm — specifically, **whether the guardrail-free oracle-Harder agreement for `lucky_guesser` (0-of-120 under Condition A) improves under B1 and under B2 separately.**
4. Variation across the 5 independent training seeds, for whichever arm(s) reach the confirmatory stage.
5. **Whether an improvement in action behavior (item 3) translates into an improvement in the actual objective (item 1/2).** These are reported as two separate rows of the same table, never merged into a single number, and a mechanism-check improvement with no MAE improvement (or vice versa) is reported exactly as that, not smoothed over.

**Decision criteria — fixed now, applied unchanged to the confirmatory numbers once they exist, never re-chosen after seeing them:**

*Development phase (informs selection only, not a claim of success):*
- Between B1 and B2, the arm carried forward to the confirmatory run is chosen by: (a) whichever shows a directional improvement in the `lucky_guesser` mechanism check (item 3) on the development seeds, and, only among arms tied on (a), (b) whichever shows no per-persona regression ≥0.05 MAE units on the development seeds relative to Condition A. If **both** B1 and B2 show a positive mechanism-check signal, **both** are carried forward to the confirmatory run (the cost of evaluating a second policy is negligible relative to training it). If **neither** shows any mechanism-check movement, this is itself reported as informative (§2.1's prediction about B1 borne out, and, if B2 also fails, evidence that even targeted behavioral diversity was insufficient) and **no confirmatory run is executed for either** — abandoning at the development stage rather than manufacturing a confirmatory result from a design that already shows no signal.
- Training-budget selection (primary 24,576 timesteps vs. the extended 98,304-timestep diagnostic arm from V1, retained here as a conditional escape hatch only) is decided from training-curve convergence diagnostics on the development seeds alone, before any MAE number from either budget is inspected.

*Confirmatory decision, on `EVAL_SEEDS_CONFIRMATORY`, applied once, exactly as pre-specified:*
- **Retain** an arm (report it as the paper's improved-PPO result) only if, on the confirmatory seeds: (1) pooled MAE is not worse than Condition A's confirmatory-seed MAE; (2) `lucky_guesser`'s MAE improves by ≥0.05 MAE units relative to Condition A (the mechanistically targeted persona, replacing V1's less-targeted "struggling_junior or overconfident_fail" criterion, now that §0 shows precisely where the opportunity actually is); (3) no other persona regresses by ≥0.05 MAE units; (4) the guardrail-free `lucky_guesser` oracle-Harder agreement rate is measurably above the 0% baseline (mechanism consistency, required but never sufficient alone).
- **Abandon** an arm if the confirmatory pooled MAE regresses relative to Condition A or Constant-Same+G, or if criterion (4) shows no movement despite a claimed mechanism-targeted design (directly falsifying the hypothesis for that arm).
- **Complexity-vs-heuristic check, applied regardless of the above, to whichever arm(s) are retained:** reported honestly against Heuristic+G on the confirmatory seeds. If Heuristic+G's pooled MAE still wins, the manuscript states plainly that the demonstrated improvement over Condition A does not by itself justify PPO's added complexity — a reportable finding in its own right, not a failure of the experiment, exactly as V1 already specified and unchanged here.
- **Oracle agreement is never, by itself, a success criterion** — criteria (1)–(3) are all stated in terms of the actual MAE objective; criterion (4) is a required-but-not-sufficient consistency check, per the explicit instruction.

## 5. Resource cost — kept bounded despite the two-arm design

- **Development training:** B1 (5 seeds × 24,576 steps) + B2 (5 seeds × 24,576 steps), each comparable to Condition A's existing ~84 seconds/seed (`training_meta.json`) — combined roughly ~14 minutes for both arms at the primary budget, CPU-only. The extended-budget diagnostic (98,304 steps), only if triggered by non-convergence, adds up to ~4× that for whichever arm(s) need it.
- **Development evaluation:** replaying the existing, unmodified evaluation procedure on the existing 1001–5005 seeds for up to 2 new policies — seconds, not minutes (no training involved).
- **Confirmatory evaluation:** replaying all four policies (including a fresh replay of Condition A's already-frozen checkpoints) on 5 new seeds — again seconds; this is the only step that touches "new" ground truth, and it is cheap because it is pure inference on already-trained/already-frozen models.
- **Total, worst case (both arms reach confirmatory, extended budget triggered for both):** well under an hour of CPU compute, no GPU required, no new human data, no new benchmark items.

## 6. Publication strategy, restated for the two-arm, corrected design

- **B2 retained, B1 abandoned:** the strongest, most informative outcome — demonstrates that the specific, quantified confidence/hesitation-decoupling mechanism (§0) is not just a plausible story but a fixable one, and that skill breadth alone (B1, the natural first guess) genuinely does not suffice, a real, non-obvious, mechanism-confirming contribution regardless of whether B2 also beats the heuristic.
- **Both retained:** still informative — suggests the blind spot's causes were broader than the single, sharpest mechanism identified in §0, worth reporting with that nuance rather than over-crediting either axis alone.
- **Both abandoned:** a genuine negative result — it would mean that even a mechanistically-targeted, evidence-based behavioral-diversity intervention did not close the demonstrated gap, which is informative precisely because it was not a blind guess; it would redirect future attention to the remaining untested alternatives (state/action-space limits, training instability) named in the prior audit, with more confidence that distribution shift alone (even broadly construed) is not a sufficient explanation.
- **In every outcome, if Heuristic+G still wins on pooled MAE:** the manuscript reports this plainly, exactly as V1 already committed to — this project's priority is a genuine, honestly-reported system improvement or an honestly-reported null result, not a positive RL claim for its own sake.
- **Permanent, outcome-independent limitation, restated:** no result from E1, under any outcome, supports any claim about real interview effectiveness, human learning, or generalization beyond this synthetic simulator. Every number produced describes simulated-persona difficulty tracking only.
