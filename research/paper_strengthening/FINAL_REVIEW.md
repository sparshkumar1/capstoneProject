# Final Review — Critical Assessment of `FINAL_MANUSCRIPT_EVIDENCE_V2.md`

Written as an independent, demanding reviewer would. No acceptance judgment is offered — this project's standing rule is that I do not make acceptance predictions, and that rule applies here as much as anywhere else. What follows is a claim-by-claim assessment of strength, weakness, and what would move the needle most.

## What became stronger, and why

1. **The evaluator claim went from a single unsourced number to a defensible, appropriately-hedged comparative finding.** The original draft's 0.796 is gone. In its place is a question-clustered reanalysis with a paired significance test that actually earns its conclusion ("not shown to help," not "shown to be equal" and not "shown to be worse"). This is now the kind of claim that survives a statistician's scrutiny, because it was built to survive one — the clustering-by-question choice is the single most important methodological correction in this pass, since treating 64 correlated answers as independent was the analytical error underneath both the original 0.796 problem and the earlier draft's overconfident framing of 0.3812 as a settled number.
2. **The RL claim is now stated as strongly as the evidence, in either direction.** No hedging language softens the equivalence finding, and no framing implies PPO "still adds value in some other way" without evidence. This is the correct posture for a result your own project has already labeled withdrawn.
3. **The speech-channel finding is a genuinely new, concrete, falsifiable contribution that did not exist in any prior version of this paper.** It is also the strongest evidence that this system was actually audited rather than just re-described: it's the kind of finding that only shows up from reading the implementation adversarially, not from re-summarizing what the manuscript already claimed.

## What remains weak, and should not be overstated in any future pass

1. **All three central results now come from very small evidence bases.** 8 questions for the evaluator claim; 5 synthetic personas for the RL claim; a code-level defect whose *behavioral* consequence on real users is entirely unmeasured. The manuscript is honest about this, but a reviewer will still, correctly, note that the paper's strongest statements are all "not shown to help" or "does not function as designed" rather than "shown to work" — which is scientifically legitimate, but it means the paper's contribution is fundamentally a validity audit, not a performance result. If the authors want a paper that claims something works well, this is not that paper, and no amount of further writing will make it one without new, stronger evidence.
2. **The dampening mechanism (Eq. 3), the bonus/penalty logic, and the follow-up gate remain completely unaudited at the behavioral level.** The manuscript states this as a limitation, correctly, but a reviewer is likely to ask why a paper this focused on auditing design commitments stops short of auditing two of the evaluator's other explicit mechanisms and the entire follow-up subsystem. This is the most obvious remaining gap between what the paper claims to be about (testing design commitments) and what it actually tests (three of perhaps six or seven statable commitments).
3. **The paper does not explain *why* PPO fails to separate from the guardrail baseline.** It reports that it doesn't, correctly, but a mechanism explanation (state signal too weak? reward imitating the same rule the guardrails enforce, per the canonical file's own note that the reward is "largely imitation of a rule oracle"? guardrails masking too much of the action space?) would turn a negative result into a diagnostic one, which is considerably more valuable and is explicitly proposed but not executed as Experiment C2 in `EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md`.
4. **The speech-channel finding's real-world consequence is asserted qualitatively, not quantified.** "13% of the confidence score's weight is zeroed" is a fact about the formula's coefficients, not a measured claim about how much this actually changes candidate-facing outcomes, guardrail firing rates, or session-level behavior on real (or even replayed) sessions. Experiment B1 in `EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md` (the full sweep table/figure) would strengthen this considerably with zero new approval risk, since it requires no code change — only more of the same deterministic verification already done once.
5. **The manuscript's central narrative — "an audit of design commitments" — is coherent, but it is also, inherently, a paper whose main findings are all negative or defect-oriented.** That is a legitimate paper to write, and it is more honest than the alternative, but the authors should go in with clear eyes that "we tested three things our system was supposed to do, and none of them fully held up as designed" is a different kind of contribution than the original draft imagined, and reframing it as anything more triumphant than that would undo the entire value of this revision pass.

## What actually supports the central contribution

The paper's contribution is best understood as: *a decoupled, evidence-gated interview-assessment system was built and integrated at real scale, and when its three most checkable design commitments were tested against existing evidence and the implementation itself, two were not confirmed and one was found to be silently broken.* This is supported by:
- A reproducible, disclosed-seed reanalysis of already-frozen data (evaluator).
- A previously-completed, audited, tagged confirmatory study (RL).
- A deterministic, repeatable code-level verification (speech channel).

All three are independently reproducible by a third party from what's in the repository (or would be, once this work is committed — nothing has been committed in this pass). None required new data collection, retraining, or a change to any frozen artifact. That reproducibility, more than any single number, is what makes the contribution defensible.

## The one experiment that would make the largest scientific difference next

Of everything in `EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md`, **Experiment C1 (the deterministic follow-up gate's behavioral evaluation)** would change the paper's shape the most, for two reasons: it is the one core mechanism this paper claims as a design commitment that has literally never been exercised against a live model in any study of this system to date (X1-B used a fixed evaluator stub that never triggered a follow-up), and it is the one place where a *positive* finding is plausible — the trigger logic is deterministic and testable, so a well-designed evaluation could actually confirm something works as intended, which nothing else in this paper currently does. That would materially change the paper's balance from "everything we checked had a problem" to "we checked four things; three had problems, and the fourth — the part actually mediating LLM behavior — held up under adversarial testing," which is a substantially stronger and more balanced paper. It is also, honestly, the most expensive of the remaining options (live Qwen2.5-7B calls, a full protocol, no shortcuts), so the recommendation is conditional on the authors deciding that cost is worth it before the next submission cycle, not a claim that it is free.

## What I would not recommend spending further effort on right now

- Retraining PPO with a different reward or state (Experiment C2) before understanding *why* the current one fails — redesigning first risks producing a paper that looks like it was tuned until it worked, which is exactly the failure mode `CLAUDE.md`'s pre-registration and held-out-evaluation rules exist to prevent.
- Reproducing the LLM-latency ablation (Experiment C4) purely to fill the empty table — it is not connected to the paper's central audit narrative, and the paper is coherent and complete without it. Include it only if the authors specifically want a systems-performance claim alongside the audit claims, not to replace what was removed.
