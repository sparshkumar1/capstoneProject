## 0. What changed since the prior plan (read this first)

`SYSTEM_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md` and `EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md` (both 2026-09-21) named **Item D** (guardrail-coverage decomposition) as the single highest-priority next step, and **Item E** / **"E1 — persona-diverse PPO training"** as the one specific, mechanism-justified retraining hypothesis worth testing, contingent on Item D's result. Both have since happened, in this order, entirely inside this repository's own registered-experiment process:

1. **Item D was executed** (`RL_GUARDRAIL_COVERAGE_DECOMPOSITION_REPORT.md`, pre-existing): found a demonstrated, direction-specific "Harder blind spot" — in 687 guardrail-free five-persona-replay turns, the training-time oracle wants "Harder" 120 times and PPO's raw proposal is "Same" in **all 120**, with zero exceptions. (This session's own prior audit pass independently re-derived this exact 120/120 figure from raw turn-log data using a different script, before this document's author had read the pre-existing report — the two are bit-identical, which is strong convergent validation, not a coincidence of a shared source.)
2. **A follow-up mechanism audit was executed** (`PPO_TRAINING_EXPERIENCE_AUDIT.md`, pre-existing): using an unattributed-but-training-configuration-consistent log, found PPO *does* learn "Harder" correctly in-distribution (72.1% correct resolution, frequent and strongly-differentiated positive reward), which rules out "weak reward signal" and "guardrail masking" as explanations and points specifically to **training/evaluation persona-distribution shift** — PPO trained only on one candidate (`persona=normal, skill=0.6`); four of the five evaluation personas are out-of-distribution.
3. **The proposed experiment (E1) was run this session**, under a registered protocol (`E1_PPO_PERSONA_DIVERSITY_PROTOCOL_V4.md`, commit `461e06b`, tag `prereg/E1-ppo-persona-diversity/v4`): B2 (persona-diversity training) was compared against B0 (single-candidate training, matching the mechanism above) on a development-only grid. **B2 did show a real, per-seed-robust improvement over B0** (dev-grid pooled MAE 0.2735 vs. 0.4389) — directly confirming the distribution-shift hypothesis's practical relevance — but this improvement was (a) budget-fragile (it degraded to 0.3942 at 4× training budget) and (b) still short of the simplest baseline, Heuristic+G (0.2674).
4. **Three further, legitimate improvement attempts were then made and all failed to close the remaining gap**: checkpoint selection across the training trajectory (`E1-Improve V1`, best point 0.2697, within noise of Heuristic+G); extending the deterministic guardrail gate to five further rule predicates (`E1-Improve V2`), which **proved** (by direct code reading plus an empirical unit test against the live `shield()` function) that four of those five predicates are mathematically no-ops — `shield()`'s own branches return hard-coded constants independent of the raw proposal, so gating on them cannot change the outcome; and matching the training/evaluation dynamics mismatch itself (`E1 Dynamics Match V1`, an 8-arm, 40-run factorial), which found that matching step size, horizon, and starting-difficulty **made tracking error worse**, not better (pooled MAE 0.3903 vs. 0.2735, worse at every one of 5 seeds).

**Conclusion carried forward into this document:** the specific, testable hypothesis this repository's own prior audit identified for the controller has now been tested end-to-end, produced a real but insufficient improvement, and three further legitimate refinements of it were also tried and failed. Per this task's own instruction ("avoid further PPO tuning without a specific testable hypothesis"), **no further PPO tuning is recommended** — not because the earlier work was wrong to try, but because it was tried, honestly, and the result is now a five-times-corroborated null (X3-A's own pre-registered equivalence result, plus these four exploratory rounds) rather than a single untested assumption.

## 1. Answer evaluation (SBERT / FAISS / CrossEncoder)

**Already established, re-verified as still current in this pass (no source drift found):**
- The full three-component composite (S1+S2+R, weights 0.15/0.35/0.50) is **not shown to outperform simpler subsets** on the 64-item human benchmark. A question-clustered (not item-clustered) bootstrap — the methodologically correct unit, since 64 items come from only 8 questions — gives R-only ρ=0.496 [0.441, 0.577] and S1+R ρ=0.509 [0.434, 0.627], both with tighter, higher-centered intervals than the full composite's ρ=0.414 [0.299, 0.587]. A direct paired difference (full − R-only mean absolute error) is +0.015 with 95% CI [−0.015, +0.051] — **includes zero**; this licenses "not shown to help," not "shown to hurt" and not "shown equivalent."
- CrossEncoder provenance: a **partially fine-tuned derivative** of `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2` (16.28% of parameters changed; encoder layers 0–3 identical to upstream, layers 4–5/pooler/classifier changed). Training data/code/split/author are not in the repository. The original Paper-2 report's "off-the-shelf, zero-shot" description is **withdrawn** and must not be reused.
- Threshold constants (`CONCEPT_THRESHOLD=0.30`, `MANDATORY_THRESHOLD=0.40`, `MISTAKE_THRESHOLD=0.55`, `BONUS_THRESHOLD=0.50`) have no disclosed robustness sweep yet — a real, cheap, unexecuted diagnostic (Item B below).
- Metamorphic robustness: MG-5 (keyword-stuffing, score ≤0.50 criterion) fails in 2 of 3 tested cases (0.578, 0.664) — the anti-stuffing "dampening" defense is demonstrably incomplete on existing evidence, not just theoretically imperfect.
- A large, 14-version-audited synthetic benchmark protocol (D1, `SYNTHETIC_EVALUATOR_BENCHMARK_PROTOCOL_V14.md`) exists, is methodologically finalized ("ready for registration"), and has **not been executed** — confirmed in this pass (no results file for it exists anywhere under `research/paper_strengthening/results/`).

**Does the composite add value over simpler baselines?** On present evidence: **not shown to**, on a small (8-question), exploratory benchmark. This is the correct, current answer; it is not yet a confirmed negative (8 questions is too few to certify inferiority either), which is why D1 (a much larger, category-discrimination design using no new human data) is the recommended next step, not a design change to the evaluator itself.

**Independent evaluation data / reproducible experiments proposed (unexecuted, see §5 for full protocol table):** D1 (already designed); a threshold-sensitivity sweep (Item B, free, no new data); CrossEncoder calibration via leave-one-question-out isotonic regression (Item A, contingent on raw-score availability).

## 2. Multimodal assessment (audio/speech)

**Confirmed live in current source in this pass (not just re-cited from a prior doc):**
- `agents/audio/transcriber.py:221`: `speaking_rate = word_count / (total_speech_time / 60.0)` — words **per minute** by construction.
- `agents/audio/confidence_scorer.py:40-44` and `agents/audio/hesitation_scorer.py:52-57`: both gate this same value on `2.1 <= rate <= 4.3`, an explicit words-**per-second** range (the hesitation scorer's own code comment says "optimal 2.1–4.3 words/sec").
- **This is a genuine, live, ~60× unit mismatch.** `rate_score` (13% of the confidence-score weight) is pinned to its worst-case value (0.000) and the corresponding hesitation term to its maximum (1.000) for every realistic real speaking rate (100–220 WPM). This has not been fixed; no results file for `SPEAKING_RATE_PROTOCOL.md` exists yet (confirmed in this pass).
- The frozen P3 simulation study is **unaffected** — it uses synthetic persona-assigned confidence/hesitation values and never calls the audio pipeline — so this bug does not change any existing paper number; it only affects the live system.

**Does the speech channel add information beyond text?** Not determinable from current evidence in either direction for the rate term specifically, since it is currently non-functional on real audio; the other four confidence-scorer terms and the hesitation scorer's other components were not part of this pass's re-verification and remain as characterized in prior audits (implemented, not yet behaviorally validated against ground truth). No claim that vocal/facial signals are validated competence measures is made or should be made — video/MediaPipe is not implemented at all (confirmed absent, per `CANONICAL_SCIENTIFIC_TRUTH_CURRENT.md` §A, consistent with this pass's file listing).

**Proposed controlled ablation (unexecuted):** the already-designed deterministic WPM/WPS sweep (Item F/B1 in the prior plan) — call the real, unmodified scorer functions across realistic WPM (80–220) and words/sec (1.5–5.0) ranges, tabulate `rate_score`/`hesitation_score`/`C_final`, zero new data, zero approval risk beyond running deterministic code. This should happen before any fix is applied, to document the defect's full shape.

## 3. LLM feedback and follow-ups (new in this pass — not previously deeply audited)

**Architecture correction, important and previously undocumented in this project's own paper drafts and task framing:** the system does **not** use Qwen2.5-7B via Ollama. `services/qwen/app.py` runs **Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct** in-process, via `llama.cpp`/GGUF by default (`GGUF_MODEL_PATH`) or via HF Transformers only when `ENABLE_GPU_RESEARCH_MODELS=1` — no reference to a 7B model or an Ollama server exists anywhere in active source. `FINAL_MANUSCRIPT_EVIDENCE_V2.md`'s abstract and this task's own brief both state "Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, served locally" — **this needs correcting before any further paper work**, independent of everything else in this document; it is a factual model-identity error, not a framing choice.

**Positive findings:**
- The follow-up trigger (`_decide_and_inject_followup`, `interview_orchestrator.py:1720-1751`) is genuinely deterministic and evidence-gated — purely arithmetic on the evaluator's already-computed fields, no LLM-in-the-loop decision point. This specific design claim holds.
- Follow-up bounding ("at most 2 consecutive follow-ups") is real and correctly implemented and tested (counter increment/reset verified at two call sites, `1873-874`/`1905-1906`).
- Error handling degrades gracefully throughout: evaluator unreachable → explicit fail-closed with `next_action: "retry_answer"`, no fabricated score (verified by a real, non-mocked-away test, `test_evaluator_failure_produces_structured_failure_without_fabrication`); Qwen unreachable → fallback to deterministic template synthesis or an honest `llm_status: "llm_unavailable"` marker, never a crash or a fabricated LLM-sourced claim.
- **The canonical-truth claim "No FeedbackValidator class exists... feedback is therefore unvalidated" is stale.** An active `_validate_feedback_output` function (`services/qwen/app.py:564-600`) is called and gated on in the real feedback path, and it does cross-check the LLM's claimed "what was correct" list against the evaluator's own `missing_concepts` before accepting the output, falling back to a deterministic template otherwise. This is a real, working safeguard that predates this audit but postdates the audit that pronounced it absent — `CANONICAL_SCIENTIFIC_TRUTH_CURRENT.md` §A needs a correction here (flagged, not made — this file is explicitly protected from this document's own edits).

**Genuine gaps found:**
- **Follow-up *question* generation has no equivalent validator** — only a length/non-empty check, no cross-check against evaluator evidence. The safeguard coverage is asymmetric.
- **`docs/FOLLOW_UP_SYSTEM.md`'s documented trigger matrix does not match the shipped code** — a "High Mastery" depth-probe branch and a `word_count<20` vagueness branch are documented but do not exist in `_decide_and_inject_followup`.
- **The one unit test that looks like it covers the trigger matrix (`test_followup_decision_policy_matrix`) tests a hand-written, inline reimplementation of the trigger logic that includes the very branches the real code lacks** — it currently provides false confidence, not real coverage, of the actual orchestrator method.
- **No per-request latency instrumentation exists anywhere in the Qwen call path** (only one-time model-load timing at startup) — confirming the LLM-latency evidence gap extends to current code, not just the old debunked ablation table.
- A raw, unsanitized candidate transcript is interpolated directly into ChatML-templated prompts with no stripping of control tokens (`<|im_start|>`/`<|im_end|>`) — a real, if currently unexploited and partially bounded (by downstream JSON-schema parsing), prompt-injection surface.

**Proposed evaluation (unexecuted, matches and sharpens the prior plan's Item C1):** a blind, rubric-based, pre-registered case set exercising the real `_decide_and_inject_followup` method directly (not a shadow copy) against its actual boundary values (0.80/0.85 thresholds), plus adversarial cases for the newly-found prompt-injection surface and the asymmetric-validator gap. Full protocol needs to be written; not started.

## 5. End-to-end system (new in this pass — not previously deeply audited beyond debunking one table)

**Session flow and failure handling — mapped concretely, with exact code citations, in the underlying agent report.** Key findings:
- Every major failure mode traced (evaluator timeout/unreachable, Qwen timeout/unreachable, empty/malformed transcript, Docker sandbox failure) degrades gracefully: fail-closed with a structured, non-fabricated response, or a deterministic fallback — never a silent pass-through of a real answer as scored, never a crash propagating to the candidate. This is a genuine, evidenced, positive finding, not an assumption.
- **One real, verified defect's fix status is unconfirmed on the current build**: `FLT-04` (NaN/Inf/out-of-range evaluator scores silently clamped to 0.0/1.0 with no flag) shows `null` (not re-executed) in the X1-A v2 rerun's verdicts, unlike `FLT-03`/`FLT-06` which show confirmed 5/5 fixes. This is open, not resolved, and should not be described as fixed.
- **A real, previously undocumented architectural gap**: in-progress session state (`InterviewOrchestrator` instances in `SESSIONS`, an in-memory dict) has no rehydration path from SQLite after a process restart — attempt-level data is durably persisted (WAL-mode SQLite), but live session state (queue position, current difficulty, pending follow-up) is not. This is inferred from code structure and confirmed absent by grep, not directly tested; flagged as open, not asserted as a confirmed bug, since no test exercises this path either way.
- **Docker deployment has zero configured resource limits** (`mem_limit`, `cpus`) at the `docker-compose.yml` level for any of the four services (evaluator, backend, frontend, qwen); only the coding sandbox itself has configured (not measured) CPU/memory/PID limits. No measured RAM/CPU consumption data exists anywhere in the repository for the running services under load — a total, confirmed evidence gap, consistent with (and now independently re-verified beyond) the prior finding that the old latency/RAM ablation table (73.22s/34.01s, 14.17GB/11.20GB) is untraceable.
- **Latency**: real, legitimate measured figures exist for the evaluator (mean 1960.8ms/median 404.6ms/P95 2098.6ms/P99 26369.4ms, n=20), SQLite (17.2ms write/5.0ms read), and `ScoreValidator` (0.004ms) — `research/results/paper1/paper1_latency_results.csv`, confirmed by direct read in this pass. **Zero rows for Qwen/LLM latency exist in this or the concurrency CSV** — grepped directly, not inferred — confirming the LLM-step latency gap is real and total, not partial.
- **X1-A is a real, executed fault-injection campaign** (60 scenario-repetitions, hashed run records, a v1→v2 rebuild-and-rerun cycle) — genuinely distinct from, and much stronger than, the discredited "fault injection 10/10" literal-PASS-string claim that `CANONICAL_SCIENTIFIC_TRUTH_CURRENT.md` already correctly flags as unsupported (1 evidenced/5 partial/4 none). X1-A's own scenario coverage was mapped precisely against the previously-proposed C5 battery: `FLT-01/02` (Qwen down/slow), `FLT-03` (evaluator down), `FLT-04` (invalid score, open per above), and `FLT-10` (empty input) already overlap C5's proposed list closely enough that a future C5 protocol should explicitly scope itself as an *extension*, covering three genuinely untested gaps instead: **malformed-but-200 evaluator JSON** (as opposed to a connection exception), **mid-session process-restart recovery** (ties directly to the session-state gap above), and **a true end-to-end zero-word-transcription case exercised through the live audio pipeline** (as opposed to an empty string fed directly to the evaluator, which is all `FLT-10` actually tests). `FLT-08` (WebSocket reset) and `FLT-09` (audio pipeline crash) were explicitly never executed in X1-A either.

**Does isolated-component reliability translate to system-level reliability?** Not established either way beyond what X1-A's real (if partial) scenario coverage shows; the session-state-rehydration gap specifically is a system-level property no component-level test could catch, and is the single most valuable newly-identified target for a future C5-style protocol.
