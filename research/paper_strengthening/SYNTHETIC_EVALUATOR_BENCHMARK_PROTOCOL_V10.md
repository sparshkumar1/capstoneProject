# Synthetic Category-Discrimination Benchmark for the Hybrid Evaluator — V10 (final)

**Status: complete protocol, NOT executed, NOT registered.** V1–V9 preserved unmodified, sample and design untouched. This version corrects an overcorrection: V8–V9, in the process of properly rejecting V4's unwarranted "population mean" claim, swung too far and described the bootstrap as offering no inferential content at all — that is also wrong. There is a real, standard, nameable source of justification for treating this as genuine (model-based) inference; it was simply never identified. No procedural change is made anywhere in this document — every correction here is about what the already-computed numbers are correctly called.

## 1. The actual source of justification — named precisely, not assumed away

V8–V9 correctly established that the 31 confirmatory topics are not a random sample from a broader population — so there is no *design-based* randomness to appeal to. But design-based sampling is not the only route to legitimate inference. **The route available here is model-based (superpopulation) inference under an exchangeability assumption**: treating the 31 confirmatory topics' true underlying effects as if they were drawn from a common, if unobserved, data-generating process — not because they were literally randomly sampled, but as a stated modeling assumption. This is exactly the same assumption implicitly required by the act of averaging across topics at all (V7's topic-equal-weighted mean already treats the 31 topics as commensurable, combinable units; naming the assumption here makes explicit something the estimator already required, not something new). It is the same logical basis that justifies random-effects meta-analysis and multilevel/hierarchical models applied to a fixed, non-randomly-selected set of studies or groups — a standard, named, defensible statistical practice, not an invented workaround.

**One additional, partial, genuinely design-based source exists and is worth naming separately:** for the three capped topics (`C_Programming`, `DSA_Theory`, `Arrays`), the 8 confirmatory questions *were* drawn by an actual random mechanism from a known, larger finite pool (25/18/10 respectively) — a real design-based randomization, though only for those three topics' internal composition, and it does not extend to the other 28 confirmatory topics, which are full censuses of their own eligible questions (no sampling occurred there at all). This partial randomization is noted for completeness; it does not by itself justify the full inferential procedure across all 31 topics, and the exchangeability-model justification above is what does the actual work.

## 2. Confidence intervals — a defensible coverage interpretation exists, correctly qualified

**V9's "not a formal confidence interval, only a sensitivity measure" framing is corrected here.** Under the exchangeability assumption in §1, the topic-resampling bootstrap (unchanged mechanics: B=5000, resample the 31 topics with replacement, average their per-topic means, per V7 §2) *does* approximate the sampling distribution of the topic-equal-weighted mean under that model, and the resulting interval has a legitimate, if conditional, coverage interpretation.

**Corrected terminology:** these are **model-based (exchangeability-conditional) confidence intervals**, not the unconditional, design-based intervals V4's "population mean" language implied, and not merely descriptive resampling distributions with no inferential content, as V8–V9 implied. The qualifier is doing real work: coverage holds *if* the exchangeability assumption is approximately correct, not by guarantee of the sampling design (because there isn't one).

**A second, separate caveat, not previously stated and genuinely new to this pass:** with only 31 clusters, and a strongly right-skewed cluster-size distribution (19 of 31 topics are singletons; three are capped at 8), the *ordinary percentile bootstrap* is known in the applied statistics and econometrics literature to have imperfect small-and-unbalanced-cluster calibration (tending toward anti-conservative — narrower intervals and smaller p-values than a fully accurate procedure would give). This is a real, additional source of approximation, independent of the exchangeability question, and is disclosed here rather than left implicit. **No procedural change is made in response to this** (a bias-corrected or wild-cluster bootstrap would be a legitimate alternative, but adopting one would be a computational-procedure change requiring separate approval, per §5) — instead, every reported interval and p-value in the eventual write-up is labeled **approximate**, explicitly citing this small-cluster caveat alongside the exchangeability qualifier.

## 3. P-values, Holm, and the family-wise error interpretation — checked exactly

**Per-test p-values (P1-i, P1-ii, P2's two components, M1):** the bootstrap tail fractions (e.g., the proportion of resamples with `mean(e) ≤ 0` for P1-i) are valid, standard percentile-bootstrap p-value estimators *of the model-based null* in §1's framework — the same status as the confidence intervals in §2: legitimate, conditional on exchangeability, approximate given the small/unbalanced cluster count. They are not, and were never claimed to be, exact finite-sample p-values under a known probability model.

**Holm step-down (V6 §2.3, unchanged mechanics: compare `p_min` to 0.025, then `p_max` to 0.05):** Holm's mathematical guarantee — family-wise error ≤ α if the component p-values are individually valid — is correctly applied here in form. Whether it delivers an *actual* family-wise error rate at exactly 5% is inherited entirely from whether the component p-values themselves are well-calibrated, which §2 already qualifies as approximate. **Distinguished explicitly, as instructed:**
- **What Holm reliably provides here, unconditionally:** a **pre-specified, disclosed decision rule**, fixed before any confirmatory data existed, applied mechanically and identically regardless of which result "looks better" — this is real, assumption-free protection against post-hoc cherry-picking of which of the two primary claims (P1, P2) to foreground, and it is the main dependable value of running the procedure.
- **What it does not reliably provide:** an exact, unconditional 5% long-run false-positive guarantee across repeated studies — that inherits the same exchangeability-assumption and small-cluster caveats as every component test.

## 4. Terminology adopted (replaces both V4's overclaim and V9's overcorrection)

Every reported quantity in the eventual write-up is described as: *"a model-based, exchangeability-conditional [confidence interval / p-value], approximate given the small number and size-imbalance of confirmatory topic clusters."* This is neither V4's unconditional "population" language nor V8–V9's "purely descriptive, no inferential content" framing — it is the accurate middle position, named precisely rather than defaulted to either extreme. Reported estimates retain their inferential character (they support statements like "the effect is unlikely to be zero, under the stated model assumption") while being honest about exactly what that character depends on.

## 5. Confirmation: no procedural change required, and why

Checked explicitly against every element named in the instruction: the sample (122 eligible / 26 dev / 67 confirmatory / 29 excluded), the hypotheses (P1-i, P1-ii, P2, M1), the scoring procedure, the answer construction rules, and the computational procedure (B=5000, the same percentiles, the same Holm comparison values, the same topic-mean-of-means estimator from V7 §2) are **all unchanged**. Every correction in this document is a relabeling of what the already-computed numbers legitimately mean, not a change to how they are computed, because the numbers were never wrong — only their description swung between two incorrect extremes (V4 too strong, V8–V9 too weak) before landing here. **If, after registration, the small-cluster calibration concern in §2 is judged serious enough to warrant a different bootstrap variant (e.g., bias-corrected-and-accelerated, or a wild-cluster method), that would be a genuine computational-procedure change requiring its own approval before implementation — flagged here as a possible future refinement, not adopted now, and not a blocker to registering the current, correctly-labeled procedure.**

## 6. Final determination

- **Claims that are justified as stated:** the point estimates themselves (topic-equal-weighted means, V7 §2, unaffected by any of this); the pre-specified, disclosed nature of the decision rules (Holm, the α thresholds, the manipulation-check handling); the scope restriction to the 67 confirmatory questions/31 topics (V9 §1, still correct, unaffected).
- **Claims that must be relabeled (this document's actual correction):** every confidence interval and p-value, from V8–V9's "descriptive sensitivity measure only" to "model-based, exchangeability-conditional, approximate given small/unbalanced cluster count" (§2–§4). This is a *strengthening* relative to V9, not a further weakening — V9 undersold what the design can actually support.
- **V9 could have been registered without material error** (nothing in it was false), **but its terminology would have undersold the evidence D1 is capable of producing**, which is corrected here before, not after, execution. **V10 is ready for registration as a prespecified benchmark whose confirmatory analysis produces approximate, model-based (not design-based) inferential statistics, explicitly labeled as such** — not a purely descriptive benchmark, and not a formally design-based one either.
