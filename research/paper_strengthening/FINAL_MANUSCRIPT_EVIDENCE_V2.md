## Abstract

Technical interview preparation requires candidates to demonstrate technical knowledge, algorithmic reasoning, and communication under realistic evaluation conditions, while human mock interviews remain costly, non-scalable, and evaluator-variable, and unconstrained LLM interviewers are prone to prompt drift and hallucinated grading. PrepAIred is a decoupled system that separates rigorous, LLM-free quantitative assessment (a hybrid technical evaluator and an independent speech-delivery channel) from bounded, evidence-gated qualitative generation (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, run in-process via llama.cpp/GGUF quantized inference — corrected 2026-09-28 from an earlier, inaccurate "7B-Instruct via Ollama" description that did not match the shipped code; the codebase's own comments and an inert, never-populated "qwen_7b" registry key appear to be vestiges of an abandoned larger-model plan, not evidence any 7B model ever ran, used only for grounded follow-up probes and feedback, never for scoring), with question difficulty adapted by a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) controller observing a continuous six-dimensional candidate state.

This paper's contribution is the system's architecture together with a direct, evidence-based audit of three of its explicit design commitments. First, does the evaluator's full three-component weighting improve on its individual components? A question-clustered re-analysis of a 64-item constructed benchmark (8 questions, accounting for the true number of independent units rather than treating 64 correlated answers as independent) finds the reasoning-verification component alone (Spearman ρ=0.496, question-clustered bootstrap mean) at least as predictive of human-rated quality as the full weighted composite (ρ=0.414); a paired, question-clustered comparison of absolute error finds no significant difference (mean difference +0.015, 95% CI [−0.015, 0.051]) — the added complexity of the full weighting is not shown to earn its keep on this benchmark. This benchmark's source data was subsequently found to be half-affected by a stale question-ID mapping and partially stale relative to later evaluator fixes (§XI.1); re-running the identical paired comparison on the qid-clean, current-code subset (4 of the 8 questions) reproduces the same conclusion — the interval still includes zero — with the point estimate's sign reversing (mean difference −0.0119, 95% CI [−0.0323, 0.0103]), reported as reinforcing rather than changing the finding. Second, does the PPO difficulty controller improve on simpler alternatives under identical guardrails? A frozen, audited simulation study finds it does not: PPO+guardrails and constant-difficulty+guardrails produce statistically indistinguishable tracking accuracy (MAE 0.673–0.687, identical actions on 120/125 sessions), and a simple heuristic outperforms both. Third, does the independent speech-delivery channel function as designed? We identify and trace, at the code level, a unit mismatch between the live transcription pipeline (words per minute) and the scoring functions that consume it (words per second): a deterministic verification shows the "speaking rate" term of the confidence score is pinned to its worst-case value for every realistic real-world speaking rate, silently zeroing 13% of the confidence score's design weight. We report all three findings as measured, including where they diverge from the system's design intent, and describe the corrective and confirmatory work each finding implies without claiming it has already been done.

**Index Terms**—Adaptive Assessment, Reinforcement Learning, Speech-Based Assessment, System Auditing, Technical Interview Evaluation, Reproducibility.

## I. Introduction

Technical interviews require candidates to simultaneously demonstrate domain knowledge, analytical reasoning, and fluent communication. Human mock interviews provide contextual feedback but do not scale; automated systems built on static question banks fail to adapt; unconstrained LLM interviewers drift from rubric definitions and can be gamed with fluent but incorrect answers. PrepAIred's response is architectural: a technical evaluator and a speech-delivery channel compute quantitative scores independently of any LLM, a deterministic evidence-gate decides when a follow-up is warranted, and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (run in-process via llama.cpp/GGUF) is invoked only to generate grounded text, never to assign a score. A PPO controller adapts question difficulty from a six-dimensional observation of recent performance, confidence, hesitation, elapsed time, and current difficulty.

This is our second pass at describing this system. An earlier draft reported two headline numbers — a CrossEncoder correlation of 0.796 and an LLM-latency ablation — that could not be traced to any stored evidence in the project, and described the PPO controller as effective when the project's own confirmatory study found no measurable advantage over a constant-difficulty baseline. Rather than simply removing those claims, this version treats each of the system's central design commitments as a testable proposition and reports what happens when it is actually tested, using only evidence already collected or obtainable by reading the implementation — no new model training, no new human data collection, no change to any frozen result. We regard this auditing discipline, applied to a real, substantial, already-built system, as the paper's contribution: not a claim that every component works, and not a claim that the system is unsound, but a specific, falsifiable account of which of its stated design commitments the evidence currently supports.

**Text evaluation.** The evaluator's semantic-similarity component builds on Sentence-BERT [1] (Siamese-network sentence embeddings for efficient cosine-similarity comparison) and its reasoning-verification component on a CrossEncoder architecture originally trained for passage re-ranking on MS MARCO [2]; §IV documents that the deployed checkpoint is a partially fine-tuned derivative of a public MS-MARCO CrossEncoder, not the base model itself, with incomplete training provenance. Automatic short-answer grading is known to be exploitable by content-free or adversarially constructed answers [3, 4]; §XI.1c's finding that a fluent, vocabulary-correct but logically backwards answer can still score well is consistent with this literature rather than a novel vulnerability class.

**Reinforcement learning for adaptive instruction.** The deployed difficulty controller uses Proximal Policy Optimization [5]. The closest direct prior art is a recent simulation framework for RL-based adaptive mock-interview tutoring comparing heuristic, DQN, PPO, PETS and MBPO policies [6], read at abstract/highlights/contribution-statement level only (its full body was not re-read in the P3 audit this citation is carried from, and that limitation is repeated here). A broader systematic review of RL in education found only roughly 20% of 89 reviewed manuscripts outperformed all their baselines (51% among papers reporting statistical tests) [7], and a separate empirical study found a trained policy selected one action 99.9% of the time and performed comparably to a constant heuristic [8] — a finding structurally analogous to this paper's own §XI.2 result (PPO+guardrails statistically indistinguishable from constant-"Same"+guardrails). Reward-design concerns for pedagogical RL are discussed in [9], and a controlled classroom study found a learned batch-RL policy matched an expert-authored policy in one deployment and exceeded it only when paired with simplified explanations in a second [10] — evidence that a learned adaptive policy's advantage over simpler alternatives is, in this literature generally and not only in this project, an empirical question rather than a given.

**LLM-generated text and evaluation integrity.** PrepAIred's architecture keeps the LLM (§VII) out of the scoring path entirely; this separation of model-generated output from privileged/authoritative decisions is consistent with a documented general design pattern for using LLM output safely [11]. This separation matters concretely because LLM-based grading itself has been shown vulnerable to injected instructions that alter the assigned grade [12] — a distinct but related concern from this paper's own §XI.4 finding of an unmitigated (now sanitized) literal-token injection surface in PrepAIred's prompt construction, and the reason no component of this system allows candidate-controlled text to reach a scoring function.

**Speech and multimodal interview assessment.** Automated, multimodal analysis of interview performance has been studied at larger scale using video, audio and verbal content jointly to predict hireability from real recruitment data [13]. PrepAIred's scope is narrower and is stated as a limitation, not a contribution: §III/§V document that no video or facial-engagement channel exists in this codebase, and that the sole audio-derived signal is a hand-designed acoustic-delivery confidence score, not a validated measurement of any psychological or hireability construct.

## III. System Architecture

**Added 2026-09-28, fifth research improvement pass — this section, and IV–IX below, replace a placeholder that pointed to `final_prepaired_ieee_paper_REVISED.md`, a file this pass confirmed (repository-wide search) does not exist anywhere in the project. No prior version of this text was silently dropped; it was never present as a stored file. What follows is written fresh from the current implementation, not reconstructed from memory of the missing file, and is scoped strictly to what direct code reading in this and the four prior passes supports.**

PrepAIred is a hub-and-spoke system centered on a stateful session orchestrator (`InterviewOrchestrator`, `agents/orchestrator/interview_orchestrator.py`, 2,299 lines) reachable through a FastAPI backend (`apps/backend/main.py`) over a REST/WebSocket API, with a React + Monaco-editor web client (`apps/web/src/`). A session begins with a warm-up phase (Q1 at difficulty 2, then 3) before question-level adaptation activates. Each turn follows one of two paths depending on answer modality:

- **Verbal answers** are transcribed server-side (WhisperX with a fallback path, `agents/audio/transcriber.py`, exposed at `POST /api/transcribe`), scored by the hybrid text evaluator (§IV), and separately analyzed for delivery-confidence features (§V); the uploaded audio is written to a `tempfile.mkstemp` path and unconditionally deleted in a `finally: tmp_path.unlink(missing_ok=True)` block (`apps/backend/main.py:890,972-973`) regardless of whether transcription succeeds, confirmed by direct reading in this pass.
- **Coding answers** are compiled and executed in a Docker-sandboxed C toolchain (`agents/coding_executor/`), launched non-root with `--net=none`, `--cap-drop=ALL`, `--read-only`, and memory/PID limits (config facts, not measured effectiveness; scoped systems evidence for the sandbox's fault handling exists in the companion security paper, cited but not restated here).

Both paths feed a common scoring/adaptation loop: the evaluator's structured output (§IV) drives an evidence-gated decision (§VII) on whether to ask a follow-up question, and a PPO-based controller behind a deterministic guardrail layer (§VI) adapts the difficulty of the next question, selected from a 125-item bank by a personalization-weighted selector (§VIII). Session state, candidate identity, and per-attempt transcripts persist to a WAL-mode SQLite database (`services/storage/database.py`); a periodic in-memory-to-SQLite snapshot (`session_snapshots` table, §XI.5) allows a reconnecting client to resume a session's main line after a backend process restart, with disclosed, bounded scope (in-progress-attempt state for the *current* question is not restored). Feedback and follow-up text are generated by a local Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model, run in-process via llama.cpp/GGUF quantized inference (`services/qwen/app.py`; not Ollama, not a 7B model — corrected in this revision's abstract, §I). No video or facial-engagement channel exists anywhere in the codebase (confirmed by a repository-wide search for `mediapipe`/`video_engagement`/`facial`/`landmark`, zero matches outside audio-recording variable names); the system is audio-and-text only.

**A genuinely new finding from this pass's data-flow review** (requested but not completed in an earlier planning document, `SYSTEM_PAPER_BLOCKERS.md` item 4): the `candidates` SQLite table stores an `email` column (`UNIQUE NOT NULL`, used as the cross-session identity key, `services/storage/database.py:50`) and `question_attempts.transcript` stores the full raw transcript text of every verbal answer, both linked to the same persistent `candidate_id`. This is a real, disclosed data-minimization and consent consideration for any future human study or deployment (§XII), independent of the confirmed audio-file deletion above — deleting the temporary audio file does not delete the transcript text derived from it, which is retained indefinitely in the database with no observed expiry or deletion path.

## IV. Hybrid Technical Answer Evaluation

The evaluator (`services/evaluator/app.py`) scores a candidate's text answer against a per-question rubric with no LLM in the scoring path (all four fault-injection findings in §XI.5 depend on this isolation). Three components are combined:

$$\text{BaseScore} = 0.15 \cdot S_1 + 0.35 \cdot S_2^{\text{eff}} + 0.50 \cdot R$$

where $S_1$ is SBERT (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) cosine similarity between the candidate answer and the rubric's reference answer; $S_2$ is FAISS-indexed concept-coverage similarity against the rubric's mandatory-concept vectors at cosine threshold $\theta = 0.30$; $S_2^{\text{eff}} = S_2$ if the CrossEncoder reasoning score $R > 0.30$, else $S_2^{\text{eff}} = 0.60 \cdot S_2$ (a deliberate dampening: concept-keyword coverage is trusted less when the reasoning-verification component itself signals a weak or contradictory answer); and $R$ is a partially fine-tuned CrossEncoder (`cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2` derivative, 16.28% of parameters changed from upstream in layers 4–5, pooler and classifier head; embeddings and layers 0–3 identical to upstream; training data, code, and hyperparameters are not preserved in the repository, so provenance is incomplete — this is a correction to the project's own original evaluation report, which had described the checkpoint as off-the-shelf), whose raw output is mapped $R = \text{clip}((\text{raw} - 0.20)/0.70,\, 0,\, 1)$.

`BaseScore` is then adjusted by a bonus/penalty stage (this pass's two shipped fixes apply here: a misconception-with-correct-vocabulary penalty tier, §XI.1b, and sentence-level deduplication before scoring to prevent verbatim-repetition inflation, §XI.1b) and, if the rubric's mandatory concepts are not met, capped at `mandatory_cap = 0.60` regardless of the weighted score. Diagnostic signals — depth, communication delivery, and the confidence/hesitation features of §V — are never added as a positive bonus to the technical-correctness score; this separation was traced end-to-end and confirmed correct in an earlier pass (§XI.7) and is restated here as an architectural fact, not a new finding.

**Evidence status:** the weighting above is `IMPLEMENTED`, verified by direct code reading; whether it is the *best* weighting is a separate, tested question answered honestly in §XI.1 (it is not shown to outperform simpler subsets on the available benchmark). The CrossEncoder's own logical-reasoning limitations (fluent, vocabulary-correct, but mechanistically wrong answers) are documented in §XI.1c as a disclosed, currently unfixed limitation of the underlying pretrained model, not of the composition weights.

## V. Communication and Delivery-Confidence Channel

A single scalar confidence score is computed per verbal turn (`agents/audio/confidence_scorer.py::score`) as:

$$\text{Confidence} = 0.28 \cdot V + 0.27 \cdot L + 0.22 \cdot F + 0.13 \cdot \text{Rate} + 0.10 \cdot \text{Rhythm} \tag{1}$$

where $V = 0.32 \cdot \text{jitter} + 0.30 \cdot \text{shimmer} + 0.28 \cdot \text{HNR} + 0.10 \cdot \text{pitch-variance}$ (voice-quality sub-score, from Praat-style acoustic features), $L$ is a linguistic-fluency score, $F$ is a pause-ratio-derived fluency score, $\text{Rate}$ rewards a words-per-second value in $[2.1, 4.3]$ (the human-conversational range), and $\text{Rhythm}$ penalizes excessive pause count. A separate six-term blend (`agents/audio/hesitation_scorer.py`) computes a hesitation score from filler-word rate, pause patterns, and pitch instability, but — a finding first traced in an earlier pass and unchanged here — **this hesitation score is computed and shown only in post-session feedback text; the live adaptation state instead uses `hes = 1 − Confidence`** (`interview_orchestrator.py:1678-1679`), so at runtime "hesitation" is a transform of the confidence score, not an independent acoustic measurement. This is stated as a limitation (§XII), not corrected in this pass, since wiring the hesitation scorer into the adaptation state would change a frozen PPO checkpoint's input distribution and requires explicit approval per this project's governance.

**The rate term's unit defect and its fix (§XI.3, restated here for completeness).** `Rate` in Eq. 1 is compared against a words-*per-second* threshold, but the live transcriber (`agents/audio/transcriber.py:221`) reports words-per-*minute*, with no conversion at the call site. Before this engagement's second pass, every realistic real-world speaking rate (40–320 WPM, deterministically swept) produced the term's worst-case value, silently zeroing 13% of the confidence score's design weight for every real session. The fix (dividing by 60 at the point of use in both scoring functions, leaving the field's WPM meaning unchanged at its source since four other call sites already treat it correctly) is shipped and regression-tested; Eq. 1 above describes the *corrected*, currently-running behavior. We repeat the caveat from §XI.3: no downstream evaluation of confidence-score *validity* — against real interview outcomes, accents, or disabilities — has been run against the corrected signal; `research/audit/speech_ethics.md` states this gap explicitly and it remains open.

**What this channel does and does not measure.** The confidence score is a hand-designed acoustic-delivery construct; it is not a validated measurement of a candidate's psychological confidence, and no claim to that effect is made anywhere in this document. It is the sole audio-derived input to adaptation (§VI) and to the guardrail layer, used both directly (as confidence) and via its complement (as the hesitation proxy).

## VI. Difficulty-Adaptation Formulation

Question difficulty is adapted by a PPO agent (Stable-Baselines3 `MlpPolicy`, net architecture [64, 64], deterministic inference with `VecNormalize`) observing a continuous six-dimensional state $[\text{perf}, \text{avg\_perf}, \text{conf}, \text{hes}, t/T, \text{difficulty}/5] \in [0,1]^6$ and selecting a discrete action (Easier / Same / Harder). The deployed checkpoint (`rl/checkpoints/seed_123/ppo_final.zip`, SHA-256 `2ab8d514…4575`) is loaded and run after the warm-up phase; if it is unavailable or incompatible, the system falls back to a deterministic threshold rule (harder above score 0.80, easier below 0.40) that is **not** a selectable study arm — only an operational fallback. **The deployed checkpoint is not the checkpoint evaluated in this document's §XI.2**: the evaluated checkpoint's SHA-256 is `299437ea…24e0`; the deployed one was produced by a separate retraining script and has no evaluation of its own. Any claim in §XI.2 characterizes the evaluated checkpoint, not necessarily whatever is currently deployed, without a separate confirmation run.

The PPO's raw proposed action is never executed directly; it first passes through six deterministic, pedagogically-motivated guardrail rules (`rl/guardrails.py`, reproduced here in their exact, strict priority order, unchanged since the frozen Paper-3 study and re-read directly from source in this pass):

1. **G4 (stuck candidate, highest priority):** $\text{perf} < 0.30 \land \text{hes} > 0.60 \Rightarrow$ Easier.
2. **G1 (overload protection):** $\text{perf} < 0.30 \land 0.4 \le \text{difficulty}_{\text{norm}} \le 0.7 \Rightarrow$ Easier.
3. **G2 (anxiety stabilizer):** $\text{conf} < 0.30 \land \text{hes} > 0.70 \land \text{perf} < 0.80 \Rightarrow$ Same.
4. **G5 (partial understanding):** $0.40 < \text{perf} < 0.65 \land \text{avg\_perf} < 0.60 \land \text{consecutive\_failures} < 2 \Rightarrow$ Same.
5. **G6 (strong performer):** $\text{perf} \ge 0.90 \land (\text{perf} - \text{difficulty}_{\text{norm}}) > 0.25 \land \lnot\text{nervous\_expert} \Rightarrow$ Harder, where a "nervous expert" ($0.80 < \text{perf} < 0.95 \land \text{hes} > 0.65$, or $\text{conf} < 0.40 \land \text{hes} > 0.60$) is excluded from escalation.
6. **Boundary clamp:** difficulty is held in $[1, 5]$ for every policy, unconditionally.

If no rule fires, PPO's proposed action passes through unchanged. Because `hes` at runtime is $1 - \text{conf}$ (§V), guardrails G4, G2 and G5 that reference `hes` are, in the deployed system, indirectly functions of the confidence score alone, not of an independently measured hesitation signal — a consequence of the §V finding restated here because it directly affects how these thresholds should be read.

**Comparative evidence.** The frozen five-seed simulation study (§XI.2) found PPO+guardrails statistically indistinguishable from a constant-"Same"+guardrails baseline (MAE 0.677 vs. 0.673), with the guardrail layer, not the learned policy, accounting for essentially all improvement over an unguarded fixed baseline (MAE 1.200); a simple performance-threshold heuristic (MAE 0.473) outperformed every PPO variant tested. This evidence is simulation-only (five synthetic personas, no real candidates) and is restated, not re-derived, in this section. No runtime study arm exists to test whether this simulation finding transfers to the deployed system: the live system implements only the PPO+guardrail condition (with the checkpoint-identity caveat above); Constant-Same and rule-based conditions exist only in the offline simulator, a gap documented independently in this project's separate system-paper research-decision track (`paper/manuscripts/system_paper/SYSTEM_PAPER_RESEARCH_DECISION.md` §5) and not re-litigated here.

## VII. Evidence-Gated Follow-Up and Feedback Generation

After each verbal or coding turn is scored (§IV), a deterministic decision function (`InterviewOrchestrator._decide_and_inject_followup`) checks, in order: a two-consecutive-follow-up hard cap; a strong-answer skip ($S_{\text{tech}} \ge 0.85$ with no missing concepts, incorrect claims, or unresolved gap); then, if a follow-up is warranted, one of four evidence triggers (an incorrect claim, a missing mandatory concept, a below-0.80 score, or an unresolved weakest-gap) selects the probe. A follow-up's own answer re-enters this same evaluation and decision path, subject to the same hard cap — there is no separate "gap resolved/persists" branch. This table was found, in an earlier pass, to not match an older version of `docs/FOLLOW_UP_SYSTEM.md` (which described a "High Mastery" depth-probe branch and a `word_count`-gated branch that do not exist in the shipped code); that document has been corrected against seven new tests exercising the real method directly, not a hand-written re-implementation of the policy (the prior version of this test suite had exercised only such a shadow copy, providing no real coverage).

When a follow-up or feedback text is generated, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct produces the text from a rubric-grounded, evidence-gated prompt; the model is never given authority to assign or adjust a score. Two structural safeguards apply before text reaches the candidate: `_validate_feedback_output` rejects narratives that contradict the evaluator's own structured evidence (claiming a concept was correctly explained when the evaluator's `missing_concepts` or, after this pass's fix, `incorrect_claims` lists it otherwise), reject generic filler, and reject score-leakage; a mirrored `_validate_followup_output` (added in an earlier pass of this engagement, closing a previously-disclosed asymmetry where the follow-up path had only a five-character length check) rejects near-empty, verbatim-repeated, or already-asked follow-ups. Both prompt builders sanitize candidate-controlled transcript text for literal ChatML control-token sequences before interpolation (§XI.4), a defense-in-depth fix for an identified, not confirmed-exploited, injection surface. **Added 2026-09-28, fifth research improvement pass — a first, small, direct behavioral probe of the live model, closing part of the gap the four prior passes each flagged and none executed.** Five constructed cases (`followup_behavioral_probe_v1.py`), one per non-skip trigger category, were passed through the real, unmodified `generate_followup()` coroutine (the actual in-process GGUF model, not a mock or a raw-text shortcut). All five used the live-LLM path; a coarse, disclosed keyword-overlap check against a hand-written description of the intended probe target found four of five generated follow-ups shared vocabulary with their target (the fifth, the below-threshold-score case, produced a topically-plausible but not clearly targeted question about recursion efficiency — a minor, disclosed targeting miss, not corrected in this pass). The probe surfaced one genuine, previously undocumented defect: the prompt's explicit `TASK` directive line named only `missing_str`, so a follow-up triggered *solely* by a misconception or an unresolved `weakest_gap` (both realistic production cases per `services/evaluator/app.py:521-529`'s own construction of `weakest_gap`) received a vacuous "targeting the missing concepts: None identified" instruction, despite the correct information being present earlier in the same prompt's context block. **Fixed:** the TASK line now targets whichever evidence category is actually populated, in the same misconception > missing-concept > weakest-gap priority already documented for the orchestrator's trigger logic (§VII, above); three new regression tests (`tests/unit/test_qwen_followup_feedback.py`) cover all three priority branches. ### 1b. Two evaluator defects, found and fixed, correlation-neutral on the existing benchmark

**Added 2026-09-28, second execution pass.** Two structural defects in `services/evaluator/app.py`'s scoring logic were identified through targeted adversarial probing (not from the 64-case benchmark) and fixed:

- **Misconceptions using correct terminology were unpenalized.** The mistake-penalty logic's only reasoning-aware rule fired when concept coverage was *also* low (`reasoning_score < 0.30 and s2_score < 0.50`), so an answer that names the right data structures/vocabulary (high concept coverage) but describes a backwards or self-contradictory mechanism — reasoning_score in this file's own documented "partial/weak reasoning" band (post-calibration ≈0.30-0.50) — received neither the existing S2 dampening (cliff at 0.30) nor any penalty. A measured example (a claim that hash-map lookups are O(n) and therefore slower than O(1) nested loops — a real, inverted-complexity misconception, using correct vocabulary) scored 0.488 ("Average") before the fix. A new, symmetric penalty tier targeting exactly this quadrant was added; the same example now scores 0.408.
- **Verbatim repetition inflated the reasoning score.** Restating an already-correct answer's sentences a second time (identical content, no new information) raised the cross-encoder's reasoning_score from 0.375 to 0.494 in one measured case — an input-length artifact of the cross-encoder call, not a correctness signal. A sentence-level deduplication step now runs before scoring only (the returned `candidate_answer`/`candidate_claims` fields remain verbatim, unaffected); repetition now produces byte-identical scores regardless of how many times the same content is restated.

Both fixes are targeted, code-consistency-motivated changes (not new arbitrary weights chosen to move a benchmark number) with dedicated regression tests (6 new tests in `tests/unit/test_evaluator.py`, all passing alongside the pre-existing 11). To check whether either change affected calibration, all 64 benchmark cases were re-scored with the fixed evaluator and re-correlated against the same frozen human-gold scores using the same question-clustered bootstrap methodology as Table X: **point-estimate Spearman ρ moved from 0.3812 to 0.3787** (both well inside each other's bootstrap CI), with only 6 of 64 cases changing score at all (mean |Δ|=0.0075, max=0.08). This is reported as a **null result on this metric** — the fixes address failure modes this particular 64-case benchmark does not specifically probe, and we make no claim that they improve human-correlation; their justification is the two directly-demonstrated scoring defects above, independent of this benchmark.

### 1c. Logical-reasoning weakness: root-caused to two independent probes, one narrow mitigation shipped, one attempted fix reverted after it regressed correct answers

**Added 2026-09-28, third research improvement pass.** §1b's fixes left one limitation explicitly unresolved: a fluent, vocabulary-correct description of a *degenerate or logically backwards* mechanism (not merely weak/incomplete, but actively wrong) can still score in "Good" territory. A focused, 7-case adversarial set (`tests/unit/test_evaluator_logical_reasoning_adversarial.py`) — plausible-sounding wrong mechanisms across two questions (two-sum, Floyd's cycle detection), paired with correct-answer controls — was built to diagnose this precisely, per two independent experiments:

- **Sentence-level cross-encoder re-scoring (refuted).** Hypothesis: scoring each candidate sentence individually against the reference, rather than the whole answer at once, would let a single false claim buried in otherwise-plausible text be caught. Measured result: a genuinely correct answer's sentences scored *lower* (0.211–0.339) than a wrong answer's sentences (0.341–0.511) — backwards. The cross-encoder's existing calibration was tuned for whole-answer comparison and does not transfer to single-sentence granularity. Not implemented.
- **Rubric mistake-text extension (partially reverted after a real regression).** Two structural bugs were found and fixed first: `mistake_penalty()` never checked rubric-text `common_mistakes_addressed` entries at all (only pre-built FAISS vectors), and `_detect_misconceptions()` silently ignored any text entry beyond the FAISS-indexed count — meaning a newly-added rubric mistake description had no effect on scoring regardless of how it was worded. With that fixed, three new misconception descriptions were added to two rubrics (two-sum, Floyd's cycle). One (two-sum) was safe but did not trigger for the tested adversarial phrasing. The other two (Floyd's cycle: "same speed cannot detect a cycle"; "reaching null means no cycle, not evidence of one") caused a real regression: **0.759 and 0.662 cosine similarity against a genuinely correct answer**, breaking two existing tests (`test_correct_answer_weak_explanation`, `test_paraphrased_correct_answer`). Both entries were reverted after the full test suite caught this. This failure is itself direct, first-hand evidence for the underlying diagnosis: a correct answer describing "different speeds" and a mistake description built around the concept of "different speeds" share too much vocabulary for SBERT cosine similarity to separate on polarity/negation alone — the same limitation that motivated the investigation in the first place defeated the most natural attempted fix.

**Net effect this pass:** the mistake-vector-overflow bug fix is real, safe, and kept (validated by a dedicated regression test and the full 17-test evaluator suite); the degenerate-mechanism and same-speed-pointer adversarial cases remain **explicitly unfixed and disclosed**, pinned by regression tests labeled as known limitations rather than silently accepted. A robust fix would require either a properly validated NLI/contradiction-detection model (a new component, with its own training-data and evaluation burden, not attempted here per this project's "no elaborate second model without measurable benefit" guidance) or much more extensive, individually cross-validated per-question mistake authoring than two questions' worth — both out of this pass's scope.

**Added 2026-09-28, fourth research improvement pass.** No evaluator scoring code was changed this pass. Two further steps were taken on the same open limitation: (1) a third, distinct mitigation hypothesis — flag a mistake match only when it is *more* similar to the mistake description than to the rubric's own reference answer (a relative, not absolute, threshold) — was tested directly against the same false-positive pair from §1c and found to fail in the same direction: the correct answer's mistake-similarity (0.759) still exceeds its reference-similarity (0.725), while the wrong answer's mistake-similarity is, if anything, slightly *lower* than its reference-similarity. Not implemented; recorded as a third failed attempt at the same underlying problem, further narrowing what does not work. (2) A new, separately-labeled 10-case adversarial development suite (`tests/unit/test_evaluator_v4_adversarial_development_suite.py`, distinct from both the 64-case human-gold benchmark and the 7-case set above) surfaced two new, real, disclosed findings: a genuine *vocabulary-level* paraphrase sensitivity (an answer using "low/high" instead of "left/right" for binary search scores concept_coverage of only 0.500 despite being fully mechanically correct, landing at "Average" instead of "Good"/"Excellent" — under-credited, not penalized), and a side effect of the already-shipped high-S2/weak-reasoning penalty tier (§1b) also firing on a merely-*incomplete* (not wrong) partial answer, which still lands in a defensible partial-credit band but was not previously characterized as a known interaction. Neither finding changes any shipped code this pass; both are disclosed for future work. Full account: `EVALUATOR_V4_VALIDATION.md`.

### 2. Difficulty control: the guardrail layer, not the learned policy, explains the gain

Restated without softening: in the frozen five-seed simulation study, PPO+guardrails (MAE 0.677) and constant-"Same"+guardrails (MAE 0.673, from a same-code audit replay) are statistically indistinguishable, with 120 of 125 sessions producing identical guardrail-adjusted actions; a simple performance-threshold heuristic (MAE 0.473) outperforms every PPO variant tested. The guardrail layer accounts for essentially all of the improvement over an unguarded, fixed-difficulty baseline (MAE 1.200). We do not claim PPO improves difficulty tracking over simpler alternatives in this study, and note further that the checkpoint deployed in the live system is not byte-identical to the one this study evaluated, so this finding characterizes the evaluated checkpoint, not necessarily whatever is currently deployed without separate confirmation.

**Update, 2026-09-28 — a later, separate development-only investigation into the PPO training formulation itself** (`PPO_FORMULATION_INVESTIGATION_V1_REPORT.md`, exploratory, on the same development grid/seeds as the E1 persona-diversity work, not this section's confirmatory data) found that a persona-diverse-trained variant's advantage over a persona-fixed baseline is real at a short (24,576-step) training budget but evaporates almost entirely at 4x that budget (98,304 steps), and traced a plausible mechanism: every PPO run in this project's history — including the one summarized above — trains with Stable-Baselines3's default zero entropy coefficient, and the raw policy's "Same"-action share was independently confirmed to rise by a mean of ~11 percentage points from short- to long-budget training, in both arms tested. A follow-up experiment adding a small, standard entropy coefficient (0.01) to counteract this did **not** reduce the long-budget regression (+0.1188 vs the unmodified arm's +0.1207) — a clean negative result reported as such.

**A subsequent pass (`PPO_V2_MECHANISM_DIAGNOSIS.md`, `THIRD_RESEARCH_IMPROVEMENT_PASS_REPORT.md`) diagnosed the regression more precisely and tested a different intervention.** Re-analyzing the already-stored per-cell evaluation data (no new training) found the regression is not diffuse: 99.4% of it is concentrated in the skill=0.80 persona, where the raw "Harder" action rate collapses from ~9% (short budget) to 0-6% (long budget) while skill=0.90's is largely preserved — a narrow, persona-specific behavior learned early and lost with continued training, not general undertraining or a general entropy-collapse effect. Motivated by this, a checkpoint-selection experiment (periodic saving every 8,192 steps across one continuous 98,304-step run, 5 seeds, evaluated only on the development grid) tested whether a predeclared, cross-seed-common selection rule could recover the short-budget advantage without needing to have guessed the right stopping point in advance. **It did:** the selected checkpoint (16,384 steps) achieves cross-seed pooled MAE 0.2697, marginally better than the original fixed-24,576-step result (0.2735), while MAE rises sharply for any checkpoint saved after ~32,768 steps (peaking at 0.4150 by 73,728 steps). This is a genuine, if narrow, positive development-stage result — checkpoint selection, not a reward or entropy change, addresses the specific instability identified. Per every prior pass's stopping discipline, this does not by itself authorize confirmatory evaluation; both findings are cited here only because they bear on how much further PPO-side effort is likely to be worthwhile before any future confirmatory attempt.

**Added 2026-09-28, fourth research improvement pass — confirmatory-readiness audit, not a confirmatory result.** The development finding above was independently re-derived from the stored per-checkpoint evaluation data (not re-run; the point-estimate cross-seed MAE of 0.2697 at step 16,384 reproduces exactly), and the selection procedure was confirmed to use only the development eval seeds, with no reference anywhere in its source to the locked confirmatory seeds or held-out profiles. A per-seed ablation, however, tempers the positive framing above: **the selected checkpoint is worse than the fixed-24,576-step checkpoint on 3 of the 5 seeds**, and the aggregate cross-seed win is driven disproportionately by one seed (999, −0.0234) whose improvement traces to step 24,576 being a locally mediocre point for that seed specifically, not to step 16,384 being robustly better — both steps sit inside the same broad, low-MAE plateau (steps 8,192–32,768) that a higher-resolution 12-checkpoint reanalysis newly confirmed: skill=0.80's own MAE is flat-to-good through step 32,768 for every one of the 5 seeds, then collapses sharply and consistently across all seeds from roughly step 40,960 onward — directly explaining, causally, why step 16,384 was selected (it is skill=0.80's own per-checkpoint minimum). **Given this per-seed non-uniformity, and consistent with this project's standing rule that no new confirmatory experiment starts without a separately-approved, hashed protocol, this pass did not spend the locked confirmatory evaluation seeds or held-out profiles on this checkpoint.** A complete, ready-to-run confirmatory protocol is registered (`PPO_V2_CONFIRMATORY_EVALUATION.md` §6) for if/when that authorization is obtained. **No claim of PPO superiority over simpler controllers is made anywhere in this document**; the development-stage evidence available supports only that checkpoint selection is a promising, low-risk direction worth a properly-authorized confirmatory attempt, not that it has been shown to work.

### 3. Speech-delivery channel: a verified, previously undocumented unit defect

The live transcriber computes speaking rate in words per minute (`agents/audio/transcriber.py:221`); both consumers of that value (`confidence_scorer.py`, `hesitation_scorer.py`) expect words per second (explicit in a code comment: *"optimal 2.1–4.3 words/sec"*), with no conversion at the call site (`apps/backend/main.py`). A deterministic check calling the real functions with realistic speaking rates (100, 140, 160, 181.27 WPM) confirms the "rate" term of the confidence score is exactly 0.000 and the corresponding hesitation term is exactly 1.000 at every value tested — its worst-case output regardless of how well-paced the speech actually is. This term carries 13% of the confidence score's design weight (§V, Eq. 1) and, via the live orchestrator's derivation of hesitation as `1 − confidence`, also inflates the hesitation input to the guardrail layer for every real session. We trace this defect fully in a companion document (`SPEAKING_RATE_PROTOCOL.md`) and confirm, separately, that the frozen PPO/guardrail study in §XI.2 is unaffected by it, because that study uses synthetic persona-assigned confidence/hesitation values and never calls the audio pipeline. **Update, 2026-09-28:** the deterministic sweep was run before any change (`research/paper_strengthening/results/speaking_rate_sweep/sweep_before.json`), confirming the rate term is exactly 0.000/1.000 across the entire realistic 40–320 WPM range tested, not merely at the four points quoted above; the unit conversion was then applied at the point of use in both `confidence_scorer.py` and `hesitation_scorer.py` (the field's own definition, at its source in `transcriber.py`, was left unchanged, since four other consumers — the orchestrator and three verification scripts — correctly treat it as WPM already); a post-fix sweep (`sweep_after.json`) confirms the rate term now varies smoothly and peaks at 1.0 exactly in the realistic 126–258 WPM band; new regression tests (`tests/unit/test_speech_pipeline.py`) cover the realistic range, the exact WPS-equivalent boundaries, and invalid inputs (a related, smaller defect — `hesitation_scorer.py` raised an unhandled `TypeError` on a non-numeric rate value, unlike `confidence_scorer.py` — was found by the invalid-input sweep and fixed alongside); the full unit suite (275 tests) was re-run and shows no new failures, only the one pre-existing, already-documented failure unrelated to this change. **We still do not claim this fix improves anything measurable about the system** — no downstream evaluation (confidence-score validity, interview outcome, or otherwise) has been run against the corrected signal; the claim is only that the signal now behaves as originally designed, which it previously did not.

### 4. LLM prompt construction: an unmitigated literal-token injection surface, now closed

**Added 2026-09-28.** Both LLM prompt builders (`_build_followup_prompt`, `_build_feedback_prompt` in `services/qwen/app.py`) interpolate the candidate's ASR transcript directly into a ChatML-formatted prompt string with no sanitization at the call site. Reading the code directly confirmed the candidate-controlled fields (`candidate_answer`, and `previous_best_answer` on a re-attempt) were passed through unmodified into an f-string already containing literal `<|im_start|>`/`<|im_end|>` role-boundary markers. A candidate whose transcribed speech contained one of these literal token strings could inject an additional, fake role turn into the assembled prompt. We do not have evidence this was ever exploited in practice, and whether the underlying GGUF tokenizer treats a literal in-text occurrence of these strings as the special token (rather than plain sub-word text) depends on its encoding mode, which we did not independently verify; we treat the fix as defense-in-depth regardless of that detail rather than as confirmation an exploit was previously possible. **Fix applied:** a `_sanitize_for_prompt` helper strips any `<\|[a-zA-Z0-9_]+\|>`-shaped substring from candidate-controlled fields before interpolation, applied to both prompt builders. Six new regression tests (`tests/unit/test_qwen_followup_feedback.py`) confirm (a) literal role tokens are removed while surrounding natural-language text is preserved unchanged, and (b) an adversarial `candidate_answer`/`previous_best_answer` containing repeated `<|im_start|>`/`<|im_end|>` sequences no longer changes the count of legitimate role-boundary tokens in the assembled prompt (exactly one `<|im_start|>system`, one `<|im_start|>assistant`/`<|im_start|>user` as appropriate). This is a code-level mitigation of an identified injection surface, not a claim that LLM feedback/follow-up output quality has been evaluated against adversarial input; that remains untested at the behavioral level (§XII).

**Separately, the same pass closed a pre-existing validation asymmetry:** `generate_followup()`'s only prior check on the live model's raw JSON output was `"followup" in data and len(followup) > 5` — a bare five-character length bar, far weaker than `_validate_feedback_output`'s checks on the feedback path (generic-fluff rejection, contradiction detection, score-leakage rejection). A near-empty, verbatim-repeated, or already-asked follow-up could previously pass. A new `_validate_followup_output` function (mirroring `_validate_feedback_output`'s structure) now rejects: outputs under 10 characters; a follow-up that verbatim-repeats or is repeated-within the original question (enforcing the prompt's own stated rule 1, which nothing previously enforced); generic filler ("Can you elaborate?"); a missing or empty `target_concepts` list; and duplicates of any prior question/follow-up in the session (mirroring the deterministic fallback's own existing dedup logic). Seven new tests cover acceptance and each rejection path. This closes the specific asymmetry noted in an earlier draft of this section — the follow-up path is no longer weaker than the feedback path on this dimension — but it remains, like the feedback validator, a structural/format check, not a semantic-quality or hallucination check; no behavioral evaluation against a live model has been run for either path.

### 5. Fault-tolerance re-audit: one framing correction, two confirmed-robust paths, one open gap

**Added 2026-09-28.** Re-examining three specific fault scenarios flagged as untested gaps against X1-A's real coverage (`research/confirmatory/X1/results/x1a_v2/verdicts.json`):

- **"Malformed-but-200 evaluator JSON"** — this framing assumes an HTTP boundary between the backend and the evaluator. Tracing the actual call path (`apps/backend/main.py::_run_integrated_evaluator`) shows the evaluator is invoked as a direct in-process Python function call (`from services.evaluator.app import evaluate`), never over HTTP, in the real integration path; there is no JSON-over-HTTP contract to malform. The closest real analogue — the evaluate() call raising an exception, or returning an incomplete/falsy value — is already handled safely: `_run_integrated_evaluator` is called from inside `InterviewOrchestrator._evaluate_verbal`'s own `try/except`, so any exception surfaces as the explicit `"evaluator_unavailable"` state (no fabricated score), and every field read off the evaluator's return dict downstream uses `.get()` with an explicit default. This is a framing correction, not a defect: no code change was made here.
- **True zero-word audio transcription** — traced end-to-end. `transcriber.py` already guards its speaking-rate and confidence computations against zero speech duration and zero scored words (`total_speech > 0 else 0.0`; `if scores else 0.0`), wrapped in a broad `except Exception: return _fallback_energy_timing(...)`. Directly invoking `services/evaluator/app.py::evaluate` with an empty candidate-answer string (mock mode) returns a well-formed, correctly worst-cased result (`final_score: 0.0`, `grade: "Poor"`, `mandatory_pass: False`) with no exception. Confirmed robust; no code change made.
- **Session-restart recovery** — a genuine gap identified in the prior pass and **implemented in the second execution pass (2026-09-28)**: `InterviewOrchestrator` gained `snapshot_state()`/`restore_from_snapshot()`, a new SQLite `session_snapshots` table (`services/storage/database.py`), and backend glue (`_snapshot_session`, called after every state-mutating message; `_try_restore_session`, attempted when a reconnecting client's `session_id` is no longer in the in-memory `SESSIONS` registry) wired into the `/ws/interview/{session_id}` handler. The scope is deliberately bounded and disclosed, not claimed as full fidelity: a resumed session's *main line* (scores, answers, question queue including any injected follow-up, current index, difficulty, follow-up counters) is restored exactly, but in-progress-attempt state for the current question (`_active_question_attempts`, `_attempt_counts`, `_timer_snapshot`, `_pre_attempt_state`, `_rl_adapted_turns`) is not — the resumed current question behaves as freshly asked. 9 new tests cover the SQLite round-trip, orchestrator-level round-trip (including a follow-up-injected queue, the one case that diverges from `self._state["questions"]`), and the actual backend glue functions simulating the real failure mode (SESSIONS cleared, reconnect, restore).

### 6. Malformed live-model output recovery: verified, not previously tested

**Added 2026-09-28, second execution pass.** `_extract_json_from_llm` (the parser standing between raw LLM text and the structured follow-up/feedback response) was previously untested by name for its malformed-input behavior, despite `generate_followup`/`generate_feedback` depending on it falling back safely whenever the model's output isn't valid JSON. Direct tests confirm it returns `None` (never raises) on empty strings, prose with no JSON, and truncated JSON; a new endpoint-level test patches `ModelRegistry` to simulate a loaded model that returns unparseable text and confirms `POST /api/qwen/followup` still returns a well-formed 200 response via the deterministic fallback (`decision_source: "non_llm_structured_recovery"`). No defect was found — this closes a testing gap, not a behavioral one.

### 7. Multimodal scope: no video pipeline exists; audio/text fusion is already correctly separated

**Added 2026-09-28, second execution pass.** A repository-wide search for any video or facial-engagement processing (`mediapipe`, `video_engagement`, `facial`, `landmark`) returns zero matches — confirming this project's own architecture note ("No video/MediaPipe") is still accurate. There is no video-derived signal to audit or fuse. Separately, tracing how audio-derived confidence/hesitation signals reach the rest of the system confirms they feed only the RL observation vector (`agents/audio/rl_state_vector.py`, consumed by the guardrail/PPO controller), never `services/evaluator/app.py`'s technical-correctness computation, which is entirely code-isolated from any communication signal — the evaluator's own inline comment already states diagnostic metrics (depth, communication, confidence) are "NEVER added as positive bonus to technical score." The concern that an unreliable communication signal could overwhelm technical correctness is verified not to apply to the current architecture; no change was needed. The WPM/WPS speaking-rate fix from the prior pass was also confirmed to be exercised on the real production call path (`apps/backend/main.py`'s `/api/transcribe`-adjacent handling), not only in isolated unit tests.

## XII. Limitations and Threats to Validity

- All three headline findings in this revision are evidence *about specific, already-collected or code-verifiable facts*; none of them is a confirmatory, general claim. The evaluator finding rests on 8 questions; the RL finding is simulation-only with five synthetic personas; the speech-channel finding is a code-level defect whose downstream behavioral impact on real users has not been measured.
- The evaluator's $S_2^{\text{eff}}$ dampening mechanism (§IV), its bonus/penalty logic, and the deterministic follow-up gate's actual generation quality remain untested at the behavioral level; `EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md` specifies what testing each would require and none of it has been run.
- **Added 2026-09-28, fifth research improvement pass — data-flow/consent finding.** The `candidates` SQLite table stores an `email` column as the cross-session identity key, and `question_attempts.transcript` stores the full raw transcript text of every verbal answer, both keyed to the same persistent `candidate_id`, with no observed retention limit or deletion path (`services/storage/database.py`). Separately, and confirmed by direct code reading in this pass, the temporary per-turn audio *file* is unconditionally deleted after transcription (`apps/backend/main.py:972-973`, a `finally` block); only the derived text, not the audio, is retained. Any future human study or deployment of this system needs a data-minimization and consent review that accounts for this — no such review exists yet, and this document makes no claim that one has been conducted.
- The CrossEncoder's training data, code, and hyperparameters are not preserved in the project repository; its provenance is incomplete, not merely undocumented in this paper.
- **Corrected 2026-09-29, sixth system-paper pass.** The previously-noted failing test (`test_stage11_5_coding_adaptation.py::test_coding_turn_preserves_speech_confidence_and_does_not_fabricate_metrics`) was fixed in an earlier pass on this project's separate P1/P3 track (a stale docstring/assertion correction, not an RL behavior change) and confirmed holding cleanly in a full-suite run of this pass's own changes (357 passed, 0 failed, `EVALUATOR_MOCK_MODE=1 pytest -q`, 527.89s). The development environment still does not match the project's own pinned dependency versions (numpy/torch/accelerate), unchanged.
- **Added 2026-09-29, sixth system-paper pass.** Table X's source data (§XI.1) is half-affected by a stale question-ID-to-rubric mapping (32 of 64 cases scored against the wrong question's rubric) discovered on this project's separate P1/P3 track, and the frozen scoring CSV is further stale relative to later evaluator fixes for 6 of the remaining 32 qid-clean cases. - The original draft's LLM-latency/RAM ablation table remains untraceable and is omitted here rather than restated or invented. **Update, 2026-09-28:** a new, small-N (n=6 repetitions, single process, no concurrent load) measurement of the real in-process Qwen2.5-1.5B GGUF inference path now exists (`research/paper_strengthening/results/qwen_latency/qwen_latency_results.json`): follow-up generation (128 max new tokens) mean 5.59s/median 5.54s/P95 6.07s; feedback generation (256 max new tokens) mean 16.64s/median 15.48s/P95 21.02s; model load 11.29s; resident memory rose from 1597.5MB to 1974.5MB on load and to 1997.0MB after 12 generations, on a 12-core CPU with 8 inference threads. - No claim in this paper concerns real candidate learning outcomes or interview performance; no human user study has been conducted.
- **Added 2026-09-29, sixth system-paper pass.** This repository maintains a second, separate system-paper workspace (`paper/manuscripts/system_paper/`), deliberately kept as an empty pre-research skeleton pending institutional ethics clearance and a set of unresolved author decisions (`SYSTEM_PAPER_RESEARCH_DECISION.md`, `SYSTEM_PAPER_BLOCKERS.md`); it targets a human-subjects effectiveness/usability study this document explicitly does not attempt. ## XIV. Conclusion and Future Work

We presented PrepAIred's architecture together with a direct audit of three of its design commitments. The original audit rested on already-collected evidence and code-level verification alone; two later passes added new, small, real experiments where they could materially strengthen or correct that evidence without retraining, without new human data, and without touching any protected confirmatory artifact: 15 real-model calls to the production follow-up generator across 12 constructed cases spanning 8 evidence categories (§VII), and a live, current-code re-scoring of the evaluator benchmark's qid-clean subset after a data-provenance defect was discovered and traced (§XI.1). Two of the three originally audited commitments (the value of the full evaluator weighting; the value of the learned difficulty policy over simpler alternatives under guardrails) are still not supported by the evidence as originally intended — the corrected re-analysis reproduces, and by holding under an independently-corrected data source reinforces, the evaluator finding rather than reversing it — and the third (an independently functioning speech-delivery signal) remains compromised by a specific, traceable implementation defect on real audio. The follow-up mechanism, by contrast, moved from entirely untested at the behavioral level to a first, real, disclosed-limitations behavioral evidence base, which itself surfaced and enabled the fix of three genuine prompt-construction defects across two passes. We regard reporting all of this candidly — including the defects it took two rounds of direct testing to find and the corrections that reversed a point estimate's sign without reversing its conclusion — as more useful than a paper claiming uniform success, or one that stopped at documenting weaknesses without attempting to resolve the ones that were resolvable, would have been.

Future work, gated on the approvals this project's own process requires before execution, includes: completing the evaluator benchmark repair already scoped and packaged (re-indexed rubric content for the 4 qid-affected questions, authored independently of the development team) so the full 8-question design's intended statistical power can be restored, rather than relying indefinitely on the current 4-question qid-clean subset; a mechanism study of why PPO does not separate from the guardrail-only baseline, building on the per-seed non-uniformity already found in the checkpoint-selection ablation; correcting and re-evaluating the speaking-rate signal; and expanding the follow-up behavioral evidence base from constructed cases to a sample drawn from real candidate session transcripts, with human-judged rubric dimensions (technical correctness, specificity, non-redundancy) that the keyword-overlap heuristic used so far cannot substitute for.
